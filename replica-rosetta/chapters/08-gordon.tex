\begin{chaptersummary}
How many random patterns can a perceptron store with margin $\kappa$? \textcite{Gardner1988} computed the answer from the replica-symmetric volume of solutions: $\alpha_c(\kappa)=1/\E(Z+\kappa)_+^2$ patterns per dimension, which is $2$ at zero margin. We derive her formula and test the stability of her saddle point, then prove Gordon's comparison inequality and the convex Gaussian min--max theorem completely, by Gaussian interpolation; with them the capacity for $\kappa\ge0$ reduces to a comparison of two Gaussian norms, $\norm{(g+\kappa\mathbf 1)_+}$ against $\norm h$, with explicit bounds at every size. Exact maximal margins at $d=100$ to $400$ agree with Gardner's curve to about $0.01$. Below zero margin the problem is not convex and the comparison gives only an upper bound; physics predicts that the true capacity needs full replica-symmetry breaking, and it is open.
\end{chaptersummary}

\section{The question: how many patterns fit on a sphere?}\label{sec:gordon:question}

A perceptron with weights $w\in\R^d$ gives the input $x$ the label $\sgn\ip wx$. Show it $n$ inputs $x_\mu\sim\Normal(0,I_d)$ with random labels $y_\mu=\pm1$: can one weight vector reproduce all the labels, and with how much room to spare? The labels can be absorbed, because the \term{patterns}\index{perceptron!spherical} $g_\mu=y_\mu x_\mu$ are again independent $\Normal(0,I_d)$ vectors. In the book's normalization the weights lie on $\sqrt d\,S^{d-1}$, and every pattern must clear the \term{margin} $\kappa\in\R$, which physicists call the stability:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gordon:constraints}
\frac{\ip{g_\mu}{w}}{\sqrt d}\ge\kappa\qquad(\mu=1,\dots,n),\qquad w\in\sqrt d\,S^{d-1}.
\end{equation}
In the proofs we write $\theta=w/\sqrt d\in S^{d-1}$, so that the constraints read $\ip{g_\mu}\theta\ge\kappa$, and stack the patterns as the rows of $G\in\R^{n\times d}$, which has i.i.d.\ $\Normal(0,1)$ entries. Let $\cF_\kappa$ be the event that \eqref{eq:gordon:constraints} has a solution. The \term{capacity}\index{capacity!of the perceptron} $\alpha_c(\kappa)$ is the critical load: as $n,d\to\infty$ with $n/d\to\alpha$, $\Prob(\cF_\kappa)\to1$ for $\alpha<\alpha_c(\kappa)$ and $\Prob(\cF_\kappa)\to0$ for $\alpha>\alpha_c(\kappa)$. It is the memorization threshold of a linear classifier, and the largest margin at a given load is that of the hard-margin support vector machine through the origin.

For $\kappa>0$ each constraint keeps a cap of the sphere, and the solution set is the trace of a convex polyhedron that avoids the origin. For $\kappa<0$ each constraint removes a cap, and the solution set, a sphere with $n$ holes, is not convex. This difference will decide everything. At $\kappa=0$ the answer is exact at every size: some unit vector has $\ip{g_\mu}\theta>0$ for all $\mu$ with probability $2^{1-n}\sum_{k<d}\binom{n-1}k$ \parencite{Wendel1962,Cover1965}, which is $\frac12$ at $n=2d$ (\cref{fig:prologue:cover}). For general margins the formula comes from physics.

\begin{physicsbox}[Gardner 1988]
For $n=\alpha d$ Gaussian patterns, the typical volume of the set of weights that satisfy \eqref{eq:gordon:constraints} is $e^{d\min_q\phi_{\mathrm{RS}}(q)+o(d)}$, with $\phi_{\mathrm{RS}}$ the replica-symmetric free entropy \eqref{eq:gordon:phiRS}; it shrinks to a point as $\alpha$ increases to the capacity
\[
\alpha_c(\kappa)=\Bigl(\int_{-\kappa}^\infty(z+\kappa)^2\Dz\Bigr)^{-1}=\frac1{(1+\kappa^2)\Phi(\kappa)+\kappa\phi(\kappa)},\qquad\alpha_c(0)=2.
\]
\end{physicsbox}

We write $\varrho(\kappa)=\E(Z+\kappa)_+^2$, so that $\alpha_c=1/\varrho$; for instance $\alpha_c(1)=0.5196$ and $\alpha_c(-1)=13.273$. How do physicists derive the formula, and for which margins is it a theorem? \Cref{sec:gordon:physics} follows Gardner's computation and its stability test; \cref{sec:gordon:cgmt,sec:gordon:capacity} prove Gordon's inequality and the convex Gaussian min--max theorem, which the rest of Part~III uses, and deduce the capacity for $\kappa\ge0$ and an upper bound for $\kappa<0$ (\cref{thm:gordon:capacity}).

\section{The physics: Gardner's volume of solutions}\label{sec:gordon:physics}

Gardner asked not what a learning rule does but what the space of all good weight vectors looks like \parencite{Gardner1988}. Its volume is a partition function at zero temperature, in which every solution has weight one and every other weight vector weight zero. With $\sigma_d$ the uniform probability measure on $\sqrt d\,S^{d-1}$ and $\Theta$ the indicator of $[0,\infty)$, the \term{free entropy of the solution set}\index{free entropy!of the solution set} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gordon:volume}
\frac1d\E\log V,\qquad V(G)=\int\prod_{\mu=1}^n\Theta\Bigl(\frac{\ip{g_\mu}{w}}{\sqrt d}-\kappa\Bigr)\sigma_d(\dd w),
\end{equation}
the logarithm per dimension of the fraction of the sphere that satisfies the constraints; the capacity is where it tends to $-\infty$.

\begin{heuristic}[Replicas for the volume]
\emph{The replica trick.} Write $\E\log V$ as the limit of $\frac1r\log\E V^r$ as $r\to0$, and compute $\E V^r$ for integer $r$ as an integral over $r$ independent copies $w^1,\dots,w^r$ of the weights, the \term{replicas}. The patterns are independent copies of $g\sim\Normal(0,I_d)$, so
\[
\E V^r=\int\prod_{a=1}^r\sigma_d(\dd w^a)\;\Prob_g\bigl(\ip g{w^a}\ge\kappa\sqrt d,\ a\le r\bigr)^{n}.
\]

\emph{The overlaps.} For fixed replicas the fields $\lambda_a=\ip g{w^a}/\sqrt d$ are centered Gaussian with covariance the \term{overlap matrix}\index{overlap!matrix} $Q_{ab}=\ip{w^a}{w^b}/d$, $Q_{aa}=1$; so the probability is $P_\kappa(Q)=\Prob(\lambda\ge\kappa\mathbf 1)$ for $\lambda\sim\Normal(0,Q)$. The Gram matrix of $r$ independent uniform points of the sphere has a density proportional to $\det(Q)^{(d-r-1)/2}$, and Laplace's method gives
\[
\frac1d\log\E V^r\longrightarrow\sup_Q\Bigl\{\tfrac12\log\det Q+\alpha\log P_\kappa(Q)\Bigr\},
\]
the entropy of $r$ directions with prescribed overlaps plus the energy of the constraints.

\emph{Replica symmetry.} Assume that the supremum is attained at $Q_{ab}=q$ for all $a\ne b$. Then $\det Q=(1-q)^{r-1}(1-q+rq)$, and the fields can be written $\lambda_a=\sqrt q\,z+\sqrt{1-q}\,\xi_a$ with $z,\xi_1,\dots,\xi_r$ independent standard normal, so that $P_\kappa(Q)=\E_z\bigl[H\bigl(\frac{\kappa-\sqrt qz}{\sqrt{1-q}}\bigr)^r\bigr]$, with $H(x)=\Prob(Z>x)$.

\emph{The limit $r\to0$.} To first order in $r$, $\frac12\log\det Q=\frac r2\bigl(\log(1-q)+\frac q{1-q}\bigr)$ and $\log P_\kappa(Q)=r\,\E\log H\bigl(\frac{\kappa-\sqrt qZ}{\sqrt{1-q}}\bigr)$. Divide by $r$. For $r<1$ a matrix has a negative number $r(r-1)/2$ of off-diagonal entries, and, by the usual inversion of the replica method, the supremum over $q$ becomes an infimum:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gordon:phiRS}
\begin{gathered}
\frac1d\E\log V\longrightarrow\min_{0\le q<1}\phi_{\mathrm{RS}}(q),\\
\phi_{\mathrm{RS}}(q)=\frac12\log(1-q)+\frac q{2(1-q)}+\alpha\,\E\log H\Bigl(\frac{\kappa-\sqrt q\,Z}{\sqrt{1-q}}\Bigr).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
This is the replica-symmetric free entropy of the solution set.
\end{heuristic}

Four steps are uncontrolled: the continuation to $r\to0$, which integer moments do not determine (\cref{ch:freeentropy}); the exchange of $r\to0$ with $d\to\infty$; the ansatz; and the choice of a minimum. At the minimizer, $q$ is the typical overlap of two solutions, which a probabilist would call the limit in probability of $\ip{\theta^1}{\theta^2}$ for two independent uniform points of the random set $\{\theta\in S^{d-1}:G\theta\ge\kappa\mathbf 1\}$. Differentiating \eqref{eq:gordon:phiRS} with the heat-equation identity of \cref{ex:gordon:heat} gives Gardner's equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gordon:saddle}
\frac q{1-q}=\alpha\,\E\Bigl[R\Bigl(\frac{\kappa-\sqrt q\,Z}{\sqrt{1-q}}\Bigr)^2\Bigr],\qquad R(y)=\frac{\phi(y)}{H(y)},
\end{equation}
with $R$ the inverse Mills ratio. As $\alpha$ grows the solutions become fewer and more alike, and $q$ increases.

\begin{computation}{The capacity from $q\to1$}\label{comp:gordon:capacity}
\step \emph{The tail function.} As $y\to\infty$, $\log H(y)=-\frac{y^2}2-\log(y\sqrt{2\pi})+o(1)$, and $\log H(y)\to0$ as $y\to-\infty$; hence $\eps\log H(x/\sqrt\eps)\to-\frac12x_+^2$ as $\eps\downarrow0$, for every $x\ne0$.
\step \emph{The divergence of $\phi_{\mathrm{RS}}$.} Put $\eps=1-q$. The entropic terms of \eqref{eq:gordon:phiRS} satisfy $\eps(\frac12\log\eps+\frac{1-\eps}{2\eps})\to\frac12$, and step~1 with dominated convergence ($0\le-\log H(y)\le2+y_+^2$) gives $\eps\,\E\log H(\frac{\kappa-\sqrt qZ}{\sqrt\eps})\to-\frac12\E(\kappa-Z)_+^2=-\frac12\varrho(\kappa)$. So, as $q\to1$,
\[
\phi_{\mathrm{RS}}(q)=\frac{1-\alpha\varrho(\kappa)}{2(1-q)}+o\Bigl(\frac1{1-q}\Bigr).
\]
\step \emph{The capacity.} If $\alpha\varrho(\kappa)<1$, then $\phi_{\mathrm{RS}}\to+\infty$ as $q\to1$, the minimum is attained at some $q^*<1$, and the volume has a finite exponent. If $\alpha\varrho(\kappa)>1$, then $\phi_{\mathrm{RS}}\to-\infty$ and no volume is left. So $\alpha_c=1/\varrho$, and $q^*\to1$ as $\alpha\uparrow\alpha_c$.
\step \emph{The closed form.} Integration by parts gives $\int_{-\kappa}^\infty z^2\phi=\Phi(\kappa)-\kappa\phi(\kappa)$ and $\int_{-\kappa}^\infty z\phi=\phi(\kappa)$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gordon:rho}
\varrho(\kappa)=\int_{-\kappa}^\infty(z+\kappa)^2\phi(z)\dd z=(1+\kappa^2)\Phi(\kappa)+\kappa\phi(\kappa),
\end{equation}
which is $\frac12$ at $\kappa=0$.
\end{computation}

Keeping the logarithmic terms of step~1 gives $1-q^*\simeq(1-\alpha/\alpha_c)/(1+\alpha\Phi(\kappa))$ near capacity (\cref{ex:gordon:near}); the script \texttt{08-gordon-\allowbreak numbers.py} solves \eqref{eq:gordon:saddle} and finds $1-q^*=5.10\times10^{-4}$ at $\kappa=0$, $\alpha=0.999\,\alpha_c$, against $5.00\times10^{-4}$. At a fixed load the formula gives Gardner's \term{maximal stability}\index{maximal stability}, the largest margin with a positive volume: the $\kappa^*(\alpha)$ that solves $\alpha_c(\kappa^*)=\alpha$.

\subsection{Above capacity: the replica-symmetric ground state}\label{sec:gordon:ground}

Above capacity the physicist asks for the weights that violate the constraints least, with the energy $E(w)=\frac12\sum_\mu(\kappa-\lambda_\mu)_+^2$, $\lambda_\mu=\ip{g_\mu}w/\sqrt d$, the harmonic cost that \textcite{FranzEtAl2017} use for jamming (\textcite{GardnerDerrida1988} count the violated constraints instead).\sidenote{The standard zero-temperature computation, written out for this book; \cref{ch:ridge} does the same for least squares.}

\begin{heuristic}[The ground state at zero temperature]
Replace $\Theta(\lambda-\kappa)$ by $e^{-\beta V(\lambda)}$, $V(\lambda)=\frac12(\kappa-\lambda)_+^2$. As $\beta\to\infty$ the replicas collapse onto the minimizer with the \term{susceptibility}\index{susceptibility} $\chi=\beta(1-q)$ fixed, and Laplace's method in the individual noise gives a Moreau envelope,
\[
\frac1\beta\log\E_\xi e^{-\beta V(h+\sqrt{\chi/\beta}\,\xi)}\longrightarrow-\min_y\Bigl\{V(y)+\frac{(y-h)^2}{2\chi}\Bigr\}=-\frac{(\kappa-h)_+^2}{2(1+\chi)},
\]
and the entropic term $\frac q{2(1-q)}$, divided by $\beta$, tends to $\frac1{2\chi}$. The ground-state energy is minus the minimum over $\chi$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gordon:ground}
\frac1d\min_wE(w)\longrightarrow-\min_{\chi>0}\Bigl\{\frac1{2\chi}-\frac{\alpha\varrho(\kappa)}{2(1+\chi)}\Bigr\}=\frac12\bigl(\sqrt{\alpha\varrho(\kappa)}-1\bigr)_+^2,
\end{equation}
attained at $\chi^*=1/(\sqrt{\alpha\varrho}-1)$ when $\alpha\varrho>1$; for $\alpha\varrho\le1$ the infimum $0$ is approached as $\chi\to\infty$.
\end{heuristic}

\Cref{sec:gordon:capacity} meets the same number from the other side: Gordon's auxiliary problem has the value $(\norm{(g+\kappa\mathbf 1)_+}-\norm h)_+$, whose square over $2d$ tends to \eqref{eq:gordon:ground}.

\subsection{Is the saddle point stable?}\label{sec:gordon:at}

Replica symmetry is an ansatz, and its first test is local: does the saddle point survive perturbations $\delta Q$ in the \term{replicon}\index{replicon} directions, symmetric with zero diagonal and zero row sums, which break the symmetry between replicas? For the Sherrington--Kirkpatrick model this test gives the de Almeida--Thouless line (\cref{ch:sk}). Since $Q^{-1}\delta Q=\delta Q/(1-q)$ on these directions, the entropic term has second variation $-\frac1{4(1-q)^2}\sum_{a\ne b}\delta Q_{ab}^2$; the energetic term contributes $\frac\alpha4\E[(\partial_h^2\log\cZ_q(h))^2]\sum_{a\ne b}\delta Q_{ab}^2$ at $h=\sqrt qZ$, as for the Sherrington--Kirkpatrick model with $\cZ_q(h)=H((\kappa-h)/\sqrt{1-q})$ in place of $\cosh$. The saddle point is stable when the entropic term wins, that is, with $y=(\kappa-\sqrt qZ)/\sqrt{1-q}$, when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gordon:replicon}
\lambda_{\mathrm R}=\alpha(1-q)^2\,\E\bigl[(\partial_h^2\log\cZ_q(h))^2\bigr]_{h=\sqrt qZ}=\alpha\,\E\bigl[R'(y)^2\bigr]\le1,
\end{equation}
the condition written by \textcite[eq.~(52)]{FranzEtAl2017}; here $R'=R(R-y)\in(0,1)$. As $q\to1$, $R'(y)\to1$ where $Z<\kappa$ and $R'(y)\to0$ where $Z>\kappa$, so at capacity $\lambda_{\mathrm R}\to\alpha_c(\kappa)\Phi(\kappa)$, and by \eqref{eq:gordon:rho}
\[
\alpha_c(\kappa)\Phi(\kappa)\le1\iff\kappa^2\Phi(\kappa)+\kappa\phi(\kappa)\ge0\iff\kappa\,\E(Z+\kappa)_+\ge0\iff\kappa\ge0.
\]
The value, $0.437$ at $\kappa=1$, $1$ at $\kappa=0$ and $2.106$ at $\kappa=-1$, counts the patterns that touch the margin per dimension, the isostaticity index of \textcite{FranzEtAl2017}.\index{isostaticity} For $\kappa<0$ the instability sets in before capacity, on the line $\alpha_{\mathrm{dAT}}(\kappa)$ of \cref{fig:gordon:phase} ($3.951$ at $\kappa=-0.5$, against $\alpha_c=4.770$). \Textcite[Figure~1]{GardnerDerrida1988} already found the replica-symmetric saddle point stable along the critical line for $\kappa>0$ and unstable for $\kappa<0$; \textcite{FranzEtAl2017} confirmed that replica symmetry is stable for every $\kappa>0$, and found that full replica-symmetry breaking covers the whole jamming line for $\kappa<0$. Physics predicts its own failure: $\alpha_c(\kappa)$ is the capacity for $\kappa\ge0$ and not for $\kappa<0$.

\subsection{Binary weights: when $q\to1$ is the wrong criterion}\label{sec:gordon:ising}

For the \term{Ising perceptron}\index{perceptron!Ising}, $w\in\{\pm1\}^d$, only the entropic term changes, and the replica-symmetric value of $\frac1d\log$ of the number of solutions is \parencite[eq.~(2)]{AubinPerkinsZdeborova2019}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gordon:isingRS}
s_{\mathrm{RS}}=\operatorname*{extr}_{q,\hat q}\Bigl\{-\frac{\hat q(1-q)}2+\E\log\bigl(2\cosh(\sqrt{\hat q}\,Z)\bigr)+\alpha\,\E\log H\Bigl(\frac{\kappa-\sqrt q\,Z}{\sqrt{1-q}}\Bigr)\Bigr\},
\end{equation}
with stationarity equations $q=\E\tanh^2(\sqrt{\hat q}Z)$ and $\hat q=\frac\alpha{1-q}\E R(y)^2$. Gardner's criterion $q\to1$ gives $1-q\simeq\sqrt{2/(\pi\hat q)}$ and $\hat q(1-q)^2\to\alpha\varrho$, hence $\alpha=2/(\pi\varrho(\kappa))$, which is $4/\pi=1.273$ at $\kappa=0$ (\cref{ex:gordon:ising}). But the expected number of solutions at zero margin is $2^{d-n}$, so the capacity is at most $1$; \textcite{GardnerDerrida1988}, who derived $4/\pi$, pointed out the inconsistency themselves. \Textcite{KrauthMezard1989} argued that the solutions are isolated points, so that $q$ never approaches $1$ continuously (their one-step replica-symmetry-breaking solution has $q_1=1$), and that the capacity is where $s_{\mathrm{RS}}$ vanishes: the script finds $s_{\mathrm{RS}}=0.314$, $0.035$ and $-0.019$ at $\alpha=0.5$, $0.8$ and $0.85$, and the zero at $\alpha_\star=0.8330786$.

\section{The stone}\label{sec:gordon:stone}

\begin{stone}{Gardner's replica computation}{Gordon's comparison and the CGMT}
\stonestep{What is computed}
{The typical log-volume of the solution set, a free entropy at zero temperature.}
{The sign of the min--max $\xi_S$ of \eqref{eq:gordon:xi}, zero exactly when a solution exists. No volume is computed.}
\stonestep{Average over the patterns}
{Replicate and average: the patterns decouple, and the replicas interact only through the overlaps $Q_{ab}$.}
{Interpolation compares $\lambda^\top G\theta$ with $\norm\theta g^\top\lambda+\norm\lambda h^\top\theta$; the covariances differ by a product of two Cauchy--Schwarz gaps (\cref{lem:gordon:minmax}).}
\stonestep{Replica symmetry}
{Ansatz $Q_{ab}=q$: each pattern sees a field $\sqrt q\,z$ common to the replicas plus independent noise.}
{One Gaussian vector for the patterns, one for the weights, nothing to break; optimizing over directions leaves two norms (\cref{lem:gordon:ao}).}
\stonestep{The saddle point}
{$\phi_{\mathrm{RS}}\simeq\frac{1-\alpha\varrho}{2(1-q)}$ as $q\to1$; above capacity the ground state is $\frac12(\sqrt{\alpha\varrho}-1)_+^2$, at $\chi^*=1/(\sqrt{\alpha\varrho}-1)$.}
{The value $(\norm{(g+\kappa\mathbf 1)_+}-\norm h)_+$: its sign is that of $\alpha\varrho-1$, and its square over $2d$ tends to $\frac12(\sqrt{\alpha\varrho}-1)_+^2$.}
\stonestep{Self-averaging}
{Assumed: $\log V$ concentrates.}
{Proved for two $1$-Lipschitz norms, with bounds at every size (\cref{lem:gordon:norms}).}
\stonestep{Below capacity}
{$q^*<1$: the volume is positive, and nothing more is needed.}
{No counterpart in physics: the reverse inequality. For $\kappa>0$, relax to the ball, exchange min and max, apply Gordon to the dual (\cref{thm:gordon:cgmt}(ii)).}
\stonestep{Negative margins}
{The replicon exceeds $1$ at capacity: replica symmetry breaks.}
{The ball contains $\theta=0$, the set is not convex, only Gordon's direction survives: an upper bound, improved for $\kappa<-0.622$.}
\end{stone}

\paragraph{What the translation shows.} Two counterparts are identities: the coefficient of the divergence of $\phi_{\mathrm{RS}}$ is the limit of $(\norm h^2-\norm{(g+\kappa\mathbf 1)_+}^2)/(2d)$, and the replica-symmetric ground state is the squared auxiliary value over $2d$, with $\chi^*=\norm h/(\norm{(g+\kappa\mathbf 1)_+}-\norm h)$. One overlap and two norms carry the same information because both computations decouple the patterns from the weights, by averaging or by comparison. The proof never continues in $r$ and never computes a volume, and it needs one idea that physics lacks, the reverse inequality, which convexity provides. Where convexity fails the stability test fails too: the two languages agree about where each can be trusted.

\section{The proof: Gordon's inequality and the convex Gaussian min--max theorem}\label{sec:gordon:cgmt}

This section proves the comparison theorem behind Part~III: Gordon's inequality, its min--max form, and the convex Gaussian min--max theorem as \textcite{ThrampoulidisAbbasiHassibi2018} state it. The only tool is the interpolation lemma of the appendix.\index{Gordon's comparison inequality}

\begin{theorem}[Gordon's comparison inequality]\label{thm:gordon:gordon}
Let $(X_{ij})$ and $(Y_{ij})$, $1\le i\le I$, $1\le j\le J$, be centered Gaussian vectors such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\E X_{ij}^2=\E Y_{ij}^2$ for all $i,j$;
\item $\E X_{ij}X_{ik}\ge\E Y_{ij}Y_{ik}$ for all $i$ and all $j,k$;
\item $\E X_{ij}X_{lk}\le\E Y_{ij}Y_{lk}$ for all $i\ne l$ and all $j,k$.
\end{enumerate}
Then, for all real numbers $\lambda_{ij}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gordon:gordon}
\Prob\Bigl(\bigcap_{i=1}^I\bigcup_{j=1}^J\{Y_{ij}\ge\lambda_{ij}\}\Bigr)\ge\Prob\Bigl(\bigcap_{i=1}^I\bigcup_{j=1}^J\{X_{ij}\ge\lambda_{ij}\}\Bigr).
\end{equation}
In particular $\Prob(\min_i\max_jY_{ij}\ge c)\ge\Prob(\min_i\max_jX_{ij}\ge c)$ for every $c$, and $\E\min_i\max_jY_{ij}\ge\E\min_i\max_jX_{ij}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The plan is to write the event as a smooth product of the coordinates, to check that every mixed second derivative has the sign that makes the interpolation of \cref{lem:toolkit:interpolation} monotone, and to let the smooth functions tend to indicators. Only the laws of $X$ and $Y$ enter, so we take them independent.

\emph{A smooth version of the event.} For $\eta\in(0,1]$ let $f_\eta(s)=\Phi(s/\eta+\eta^{-1/2})$: it is infinitely differentiable and nondecreasing, with values in $(0,1)$ and bounded derivatives of all orders, and $f_\eta(s)\to\ind\{s\ge0\}$ for every $s$ as $\eta\downarrow0$. For $x=(x_{ij})$ put $u_{ij}=f_\eta(x_{ij}-\lambda_{ij})$, $A_i=1-\prod_{p=1}^J(1-u_{ip})$ and $F(x)=\prod_{i=1}^IA_i$. As a polynomial in the $u_{ij}$, $F$ is infinitely differentiable with bounded derivatives, and $0\le F\le1$.

\emph{The signs of the second derivatives.} Write $f'_{ij}=f_\eta'(x_{ij}-\lambda_{ij})\ge0$. Then $\partial_{ij}F=f'_{ij}\prod_{p\ne j}(1-u_{ip})\prod_{l\ne i}A_l$. Differentiating once more, for $k\ne j$ in the same row and for $l\ne i$ in another row,
\begin{gather*}
\partial_{ik}\partial_{ij}F=-f'_{ij}f'_{ik}\prod_{p\ne j,k}(1-u_{ip})\prod_{l\ne i}A_l\le0,\\
\partial_{lk}\partial_{ij}F=f'_{ij}f'_{lk}\prod_{p\ne j}(1-u_{ip})\prod_{p\ne k}(1-u_{lp})\prod_{m\ne i,l}A_m\ge0,
\end{gather*}
because all the factors $u$, $1-u$ and $A$ lie in $[0,1]$.

\emph{Interpolation.} By \cref{lem:toolkit:interpolation}, with $Z_t=\sqrt t\,X+\sqrt{1-t}\,Y$,
\[
\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\E F(Z_t)=\frac12\sum_{(i,j),(l,k)}\bigl(\E X_{ij}X_{lk}-\E Y_{ij}Y_{lk}\bigr)\,\E\bigl[\partial_{ij}\partial_{lk}F(Z_t)\bigr].
\]
We check the sign term by term. The diagonal terms vanish by hypothesis (i). For $l=i$ and $k\ne j$ the covariance difference is nonnegative by (ii) and the derivative nonpositive; for $l\ne i$ the difference is nonpositive by (iii) and the derivative nonnegative. So $\E F(X)=\E F(Z_1)\le\E F(Z_0)=\E F(Y)$.

\emph{The limit.} As $\eta\downarrow0$, $u_{ij}\to\ind\{x_{ij}\ge\lambda_{ij}\}$, so $F(x)$ tends to $\prod_i(1-\prod_j\ind\{x_{ij}<\lambda_{ij}\})$, the indicator of the event in \eqref{eq:gordon:gordon}, at every $x$; bounded convergence gives \eqref{eq:gordon:gordon}. With $\lambda_{ij}=c$ the events are $\{\min_i\max_jY_{ij}\ge c\}$ and $\{\min_i\max_jX_{ij}\ge c\}$, and the expectations compare because $\E W=\int_0^\infty\Prob(W\ge c)\dd c-\int_{-\infty}^0\Prob(W<c)\dd c$ for an integrable $W$.
\end{proof}

With one row ($I=1$), hypothesis (iii) is empty and the theorem is Slepian's lemma, \cref{thm:toolkit:slepian}(i)\index{Slepian's lemma}; Gordon's inequality lets the two levels compare in opposite directions. \Textcite{Gordon1985} proved it as a generalization of the inequalities of Slepian and Fernique; the interpolation proof is standard. For learning, the bilinear form matters most.

\begin{lemma}[Gordon's min--max inequality]\label{lem:gordon:minmax}
Let $G\in\R^{n\times d}$, $g\in\R^n$, $h\in\R^d$ and $z\in\R$ be independent, with i.i.d.\ $\Normal(0,1)$ entries. Let $S_w\subset\R^d$ and $S_u\subset\R^n$ be nonempty compact sets and $\psi$ a continuous function on $S_w\times S_u$. Define
\begin{align*}
\cP(G,z)&=\min_{w\in S_w}\max_{u\in S_u}\bigl(u^\top Gw+z\norm w\norm u+\psi(w,u)\bigr),\\
\cA(g,h)&=\min_{w\in S_w}\max_{u\in S_u}\bigl(\norm w\,g^\top u+\norm u\,h^\top w+\psi(w,u)\bigr).
\end{align*}
Then $\Prob(\cP(G,z)<c)\le\Prob(\cA(g,h)\le c)$ for every $c\in\R$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The plan is to apply \cref{thm:gordon:gordon} on finite subsets, with rows indexed by $w$ and columns by $u$, and to pass to the compact sets by approximation.

\emph{Finite sets.} For finite $W\subset S_w$, $U\subset S_u$ and $(w,u)\in W\times U$, let $X_{wu}=\norm w\,g^\top u+\norm u\,h^\top w$ and $Y_{wu}=u^\top Gw+z\norm w\norm u$. Since $\E(u^\top Gw)(u'^\top Gw')=\ip u{u'}\ip w{w'}$, $\E(g^\top u)(g^\top u')=\ip u{u'}$ and $\E(h^\top w)(h^\top w')=\ip w{w'}$, expanding the products gives
\[
\E X_{wu}X_{w'u'}-\E Y_{wu}Y_{w'u'}=-\bigl(\norm w\norm{w'}-\ip w{w'}\bigr)\bigl(\norm u\norm{u'}-\ip u{u'}\bigr).
\]
Both factors are nonnegative by the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, and the first vanishes when $w=w'$; so hypotheses (i) and (ii) of \cref{thm:gordon:gordon} hold with equality, and (iii) holds. With $\lambda_{wu}=c-\psi(w,u)$ the theorem gives $\Prob(\cP_{W,U}\ge c)\ge\Prob(\cA_{W,U}\ge c)$ for the values of the problems restricted to $W\times U$, that is, $\Prob(\cP_{W,U}<c)\le\Prob(\cA_{W,U}<c)$.

\emph{Compact sets.} For $k\ge1$ let $W_k\subset S_w$ and $U_k\subset S_u$ be finite sets within distance $1/k$ of every point of $S_w$ and of $S_u$. For a fixed realization the objective $F(w,u)=u^\top Gw+z\norm w\norm u+\psi(w,u)$ is uniformly continuous on the compact $S_w\times S_u$, with a modulus of continuity $\omega$. Then $M(w)=\max_{S_u}F(w,\cdot)$ and $M_k(w)=\max_{U_k}F(w,\cdot)$ satisfy $\abs{M_k-M}\le\omega(1/k)$, and $M$ has modulus $\omega$, so $\abs{\min_{W_k}M_k-\min_{S_w}M}\le2\omega(1/k)$. Hence $\cP_k=\cP_{W_k,U_k}\to\cP(G,z)$ for every realization, and likewise $\cA_k\to\cA(g,h)$. If $\cP(G,z)<c$, then $\cP_k<c$ for all large $k$; if $\cA_k<c$ for infinitely many $k$, then $\cA(g,h)\le c$. Fatou's lemma, applied to indicators, gives
\[
\Prob(\cP(G,z)<c)\le\liminf_k\Prob(\cP_k<c)\le\limsup_k\Prob(\cA_k<c)\le\Prob(\cA(g,h)\le c).\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

The term $z\norm w\norm u$ only equalizes the variances. It can be removed at the price of a factor $2$, and convexity then buys the reverse inequality.\index{convex Gaussian min--max theorem}

\begin{theorem}[convex Gaussian min--max theorem; \textcite{ThrampoulidisAbbasiHassibi2018}, Theorem~6.1]\label{thm:gordon:cgmt}
Let $G\in\R^{n\times d}$, $g\in\R^n$ and $h\in\R^d$ have i.i.d.\ $\Normal(0,1)$ entries, let $S_w\subset\R^d$ and $S_u\subset\R^n$ be compact sets, and let $\psi$ be continuous on $S_w\times S_u$. Define the primary and the auxiliary optimization problems
\begin{align}
\cP(G)&=\min_{w\in S_w}\max_{u\in S_u}\bigl(u^\top Gw+\psi(w,u)\bigr),\label{eq:gordon:po}\\
\cA(g,h)&=\min_{w\in S_w}\max_{u\in S_u}\bigl(\norm w\,g^\top u+\norm u\,h^\top w+\psi(w,u)\bigr),\label{eq:gordon:ao}
\end{align}
and let $w_{\cP}(G)$ denote a minimizer in \eqref{eq:gordon:po}. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item for every $c\in\R$, $\Prob(\cP(G)<c)\le2\Prob(\cA(g,h)\le c)$;
\item if, in addition, $S_w$ and $S_u$ are convex and $\psi$ is convex--concave on $S_w\times S_u$, then for every $c\in\R$, $\Prob(\cP(G)>c)\le2\Prob(\cA(g,h)\ge c)$; in particular, for all $\mu\in\R$ and $t>0$, $\Prob(\abs{\cP(G)-\mu}>t)\le2\Prob(\abs{\cA(g,h)-\mu}\ge t)$;
\item under the assumptions of (ii), let $S$ be an open subset of $S_w$ and $S^c=S_w\setminus S$, and let $\cP_{S^c}(G)$ and $\cA_{S^c}(g,h)$ be the optimal values of \eqref{eq:gordon:po} and \eqref{eq:gordon:ao} with the minimization restricted to $w\in S^c$. If there are constants $\bar a$, $\bar a_{S^c}$ and $\eta>0$ such that (a) $\bar a_{S^c}\ge\bar a+3\eta$, (b) $\cA(g,h)<\bar a+\eta$ with probability at least $1-p$, and (c) $\cA_{S^c}(g,h)>\bar a_{S^c}-\eta$ with probability at least $1-p$, then $\Prob(w_{\cP}(G)\in S)\ge1-4p$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[asymptotic CGMT; \textcite{ThrampoulidisAbbasiHassibi2018}, Corollary~6.1]\label{cor:gordon:asymptotic}
Consider a sequence of problems as in \cref{thm:gordon:cgmt}(iii), indexed by $d$, with $n=n(d)$. If there are constants $\bar a<\bar a_{S^c}$ such that $\cA(g,h)\to\bar a$ and $\cA_{S^c}(g,h)\to\bar a_{S^c}$ in probability, then $\Prob(w_{\cP}(G)\in S)\to1$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:gordon:cgmt} and \cref{cor:gordon:asymptotic}]
The plan is to remove the term $z\norm w\norm u$ of \cref{lem:gordon:minmax} by conditioning on its sign, to obtain (ii) by applying (i) to the dual problem, and (iii) by comparing two optimal values.

(i) Let $z\sim\Normal(0,1)$ be independent of $G$. On $\{z\le0\}$ the objective of $\cP(G,z)$ is pointwise at most that of $\cP(G)$, so $\cP(G,z)\le\cP(G)$; by independence and \cref{lem:gordon:minmax},
\[
\tfrac12\Prob(\cP(G)<c)=\Prob(\cP(G)<c,\ z\le0)\le\Prob(\cP(G,z)<c)\le\Prob(\cA(g,h)\le c).
\]

(ii) The function $(w,u)\mapsto u^\top Gw+\psi(w,u)$ is continuous on the compact convex set $S_w\times S_u$, convex in $w$ and concave in $u$, so Sion's minimax theorem \parencite{Sion1958} gives $\cP(G)=\max_u\min_w(u^\top Gw+\psi(w,u))$, that is,
\[
-\cP(G)=\min_{u\in S_u}\max_{w\in S_w}\bigl(w^\top(-G^\top)u-\psi(w,u)\bigr),
\]
a primary problem with the roles of $w$ and $u$ exchanged, the matrix $-G^\top$ with i.i.d.\ standard normal entries, and the continuous function $-\psi$. Its auxiliary problem, with standard Gaussian vectors $\tilde g\in\R^d$ and $\tilde h\in\R^n$, is $\min_u\max_w(\norm u\,\tilde g^\top w+\norm w\,\tilde h^\top u-\psi(w,u))$. Since $(\tilde g,\tilde h)$ has the law of $(-h,-g)$, this has the law of
\[
-\max_{u\in S_u}\min_{w\in S_w}\bigl(\norm w\,g^\top u+\norm u\,h^\top w+\psi(w,u)\bigr).
\]
Part (i) at the level $-c$ gives $\Prob(\cP(G)>c)\le2\Prob(\max_u\min_w(\cdots)\ge c)$, and the weak minimax inequality $\max_u\min_w\le\min_w\max_u$, which needs no convexity, bounds the right side by $2\Prob(\cA(g,h)\ge c)$. For the last claim, add (i) at $c=\mu-t$ and (ii) at $c=\mu+t$.

(iii) If $S^c$ is empty there is nothing to prove. Otherwise $S^c$ is compact, so (i) applies to the restricted problems and, with (c), gives $\Prob(\cP_{S^c}(G)<\bar a_{S^c}-\eta)\le2p$; by (ii) and (b), $\Prob(\cP(G)>\bar a+\eta)\le2p$. Outside these two events, so with probability at least $1-4p$, assumption (a) gives
\[
\cP_{S^c}(G)\ge\bar a_{S^c}-\eta\ge\bar a+2\eta>\bar a+\eta\ge\cP(G).
\]
A minimizer $w_{\cP}$ in $S^c$ would give $\cP(G)=\max_u(u^\top Gw_{\cP}+\psi(w_{\cP},u))\ge\cP_{S^c}(G)$, which this chain excludes; so every minimizer lies in $S$. For the corollary put $\eta=(\bar a_{S^c}-\bar a)/3$: for every $p>0$, (b) and (c) hold with probability at least $1-p$ for all large $d$, and (iii) applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[how the theorem is used]\label{rem:gordon:use}
Part (i) needs no convexity: the auxiliary value is a high-probability lower bound for the primary one. Part (ii) adds the upper bound, so that if $\cA(g,h)$ concentrates, so does $\cP(G)$. Part (iii) localizes minimizers, typically in a shell $\{\abs{\norm w-a_*}<\eps\}$; it is proved from (ii), so the convexity hypotheses are part of it, as in \textcite[Theorem~3]{ThrampoulidisOymakHassibi2015}; \textcite{ThrampoulidisAbbasiHassibi2018} do not repeat them in the statement of (iii). In applications one first confines the minimizer to a large ball (\cref{ch:ridge}). The idea of combining Gordon's inequality with convex duality is Stojnic's \parencite{Stojnic2013d}; \textcite{ThrampoulidisOymakHassibi2015} first stated parts (i)--(ii), and \textcite{ThrampoulidisAbbasiHassibi2018} gave (iii) in this generality and credit Stojnic explicitly.
\end{remark}

\section{The proof: the capacity for nonnegative margins}\label{sec:gordon:capacity}

We prove Gardner's formula for $\kappa\ge0$ and the upper bound for every $\kappa$, following \textcite{Stojnic2013a} with the CGMT in place of his direct use of Gordon's inequality, and with bounds at every size. Write $\Lambda=\{\lambda\in\R^n:\lambda\ge0,\ \norm\lambda\le1\}$, $B^d$ for the closed unit ball, and, for independent $g\sim\Normal(0,I_n)$ and $h\sim\Normal(0,I_d)$, $D_\kappa=\norm{(g+\kappa\mathbf 1)_+}-\norm h$. For $v\in\R^n$ and $\lambda\in\Lambda$, $\lambda^\top v\le\lambda^\top v_+\le\norm\lambda\norm{v_+}$, with equality at $\lambda=v_+/\norm{v_+}$ when $v_+\ne0$; so $\max_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\lambda^\top v=\norm{v_+}$, and $\cF_\kappa$ is the event that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gordon:xi}
\xi_S=\min_{\theta\in S^{d-1}}\max_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\lambda^\top(\kappa\mathbf 1-G\theta)=\min_{\theta\in S^{d-1}}\norm{(\kappa\mathbf 1-G\theta)_+}
\end{equation}
vanishes: a primary problem with $w=\theta$, $u=\lambda$, the matrix $-G$ and $\psi(\theta,\lambda)=\kappa\mathbf 1^\top\lambda$.

\begin{lemma}[the auxiliary problems of the perceptron]\label{lem:gordon:ao}
Let $\kappa\in\R$, $g\in\R^n$, $h\in\R^d$, $v=g+\kappa\mathbf 1$ and $D=\norm{v_+}-\norm h$. Let $\cA_S$ and $\cA_B$ be the values of
\[
\min_\theta\max_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\bigl(\norm\theta\,g^\top\lambda+\norm\lambda\,h^\top\theta+\kappa\mathbf 1^\top\lambda\bigr)
\]
with $\theta$ ranging over $S^{d-1}$ and over $B^d$. If $v_+\ne0$, then $\cA_S\ge D_+$; in all cases $\{\cA_S\le0\}\subseteq\{D\le0\}$ and $\cA_B\le D_+$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $\theta\in S^{d-1}$ the objective is $\lambda^\top v+\norm\lambda\,h^\top\theta$. If $v_+\ne0$, the choice $\lambda=v_+/\norm{v_+}$ gives $\norm{v_+}+h^\top\theta\ge D$ and $\lambda=0$ gives $0$, so the inner maximum is at least $D_+$ for every $\theta$. Then $\cA_S\le0$ forces $D\le0$; and if $v_+=0$, then $D=-\norm h\le0$ anyway. For $\cA_B$ take $\theta=-h/\norm h$ (an arbitrary unit vector if $h=0$); for every $\lambda\in\Lambda$ the objective is $\lambda^\top v-\norm\lambda\norm h\le\norm\lambda(\norm{v_+}-\norm h)\le D_+$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[two Gaussian norms]\label{lem:gordon:norms}
Let $\varrho=\varrho(\kappa)$. Then $\Var D_\kappa\le2$. If $n\varrho>d+1$, then $\Prob(D_\kappa\le0)\le2/(\sqrt{n\varrho-1}-\sqrt d)^2$; if $n\varrho<d-1$, then $\Prob(D_\kappa>0)\le2/(\sqrt{d-1}-\sqrt{n\varrho})^2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The maps $g\mapsto\norm{(g+\kappa\mathbf 1)_+}$ and $h\mapsto\norm h$ are $1$-Lipschitz, so each has variance at most $1$ by \cref{thm:toolkit:poincare}(ii); they are independent, so $\Var D_\kappa\le2$. Since $\E\norm{(g+\kappa\mathbf 1)_+}^2=n\varrho$, $(\E\norm{(g+\kappa\mathbf 1)_+})^2=n\varrho-\Var\norm{(g+\kappa\mathbf 1)_+}\in[n\varrho-1,n\varrho]$, and likewise $\E\norm h\in[\sqrt{d-1},\sqrt d]$. If $n\varrho>d+1$, then $\E D_\kappa\ge\sqrt{n\varrho-1}-\sqrt d>0$, and Chebyshev's inequality bounds $\Prob(D_\kappa\le0)$ by $\Var D_\kappa/(\E D_\kappa)^2$. If $n\varrho<d-1$, then $-\E D_\kappa\ge\sqrt{d-1}-\sqrt{n\varrho}>0$, and the same inequality bounds $\Prob(D_\kappa>0)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[the capacity of the spherical perceptron]\label{thm:gordon:capacity}
Let $G\in\R^{n\times d}$ have i.i.d.\ $\Normal(0,1)$ rows $g_1,\dots,g_n$; for $\kappa\in\R$ let $\cF_\kappa$ be the event that some $\theta\in S^{d-1}$ satisfies $\ip{g_\mu}\theta\ge\kappa$ for all $\mu\le n$; and let $\varrho(\kappa)=\E(Z+\kappa)_+^2$ and $\alpha_c=1/\varrho$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item (Every margin.) For every $\kappa\in\R$, $\Prob(\cF_\kappa)\le2\Prob(D_\kappa\le0)$; and if $n\varrho(\kappa)>d+1$, then $\Prob(\cF_\kappa)\le4/(\sqrt{n\varrho(\kappa)-1}-\sqrt d)^2$.
\item (Nonnegative margins.) For every $\kappa\ge0$, $\Prob(\cF_\kappa^c)\le2\Prob(D_\kappa>0)$; and if $n\varrho(\kappa)<d-1$, then $\Prob(\cF_\kappa^c)\le4/(\sqrt{d-1}-\sqrt{n\varrho(\kappa)})^2$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, if $n,d\to\infty$ with $n/d\to\alpha\in(0,\infty)$, then $\Prob(\cF_\kappa)\to0$ for every $\kappa$ with $\alpha>\alpha_c(\kappa)$, and $\Prob(\cF_\kappa)\to1$ for every $\kappa\ge0$ with $\alpha<\alpha_c(\kappa)$. In particular the capacity at zero margin is $2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The plan is to read $\cF_\kappa$ as the vanishing of a primary problem, to bound its probability from above with part (i) of the CGMT on the sphere and from below with part (ii) on the ball, and to evaluate both auxiliary problems with \cref{lem:gordon:ao,lem:gordon:norms}.

(i) The value $\xi_S$ of \eqref{eq:gordon:xi} is the primary value $\cP(-G)$ of \cref{thm:gordon:cgmt} with the compact sets $S_w=S^{d-1}$ and $S_u=\Lambda$ and the continuous function $\psi(\theta,\lambda)=\kappa\mathbf 1^\top\lambda$; the matrix $-G$ has i.i.d.\ standard normal entries, and on the sphere $\norm\theta=1$, so the auxiliary problem is $\cA_S$. Since $\xi_S\ge0$, part (i) gives, for every $c>0$,
\[
\Prob(\cF_\kappa)=\Prob(\xi_S\le0)\le\Prob(\xi_S<c)\le2\Prob(\cA_S\le c).
\]
Letting $c\downarrow0$ and using \cref{lem:gordon:ao} gives $\Prob(\cF_\kappa)\le2\Prob(\cA_S\le0)\le2\Prob(D_\kappa\le0)$, and \cref{lem:gordon:norms} gives the explicit bound.

(ii) Let first $\kappa>0$. Then $\cF_\kappa$ is the event that some $\theta\in B^d$ has $G\theta\ge\kappa\mathbf 1$: such a $\theta$ is nonzero, and $\theta/\norm\theta$ satisfies $G\theta/\norm\theta\ge\kappa\mathbf 1/\norm\theta\ge\kappa\mathbf 1$. So $\cF_\kappa=\{\xi_B=0\}$ with $\xi_B=\min_{\theta\in B^d}\max_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\lambda^\top(\kappa\mathbf 1-G\theta)$, a primary problem with compact convex sets and a bilinear-plus-linear, hence convex--concave, objective. Part (ii) of the CGMT and \cref{lem:gordon:ao} give, for every $c>0$,
\[
\Prob(\xi_B>c)\le2\Prob(\cA_B\ge c)\le2\Prob(D_\kappa\ge c).
\]
As $c\downarrow0$ the left side increases to $\Prob(\cF_\kappa^c)$ and the right side decreases to $2\Prob(D_\kappa>0)$. For $\kappa=0$, note that $\cF_0\supseteq\cF_{\kappa'}$ for every $\kappa'>0$, so $\Prob(\cF_0^c)\le2\Prob(D_{\kappa'}>0)$. As $\kappa'\downarrow0$, $D_{\kappa'}$ decreases to $D_0$, so the events $\{D_{\kappa'}>0\}$ decrease to an event contained in $\{D_0\ge0\}$; and $\Prob(D_0=0)=0$, because $\norm h$ has a density and is independent of $g$. Hence $\Prob(\cF_0^c)\le2\Prob(D_0>0)$, and \cref{lem:gordon:norms} gives the explicit bound.

For the limits: if $\alpha>\alpha_c(\kappa)$ then $(\sqrt{n\varrho-1}-\sqrt d)^2/d\to(\sqrt{\alpha\varrho}-1)^2>0$, and if $\kappa\ge0$ and $\alpha<\alpha_c(\kappa)$ then $(\sqrt{d-1}-\sqrt{n\varrho})^2/d\to(1-\sqrt{\alpha\varrho})^2>0$; in both cases the bound is $O(1/d)$.
\end{proof}

The bounds locate the transition within $\abs{n\varrho(\kappa)-d}=O(\sqrt d)$, the width of Cover's window at $\kappa=0$; with the sharp concentration inequality, \cref{thm:toolkit:lipschitz}(ii), applied to the $\sqrt2$-Lipschitz function $D_\kappa$, they become exponential, for instance $\Prob(\cF_\kappa)\le2\exp(-(\sqrt{n\varrho-1}-\sqrt d)^2/4)$.

\begin{corollary}[the largest margin]\label{cor:gordon:margin}
Let $\kappa_{\max}(G)=\max_{\theta\in S^{d-1}}\min_\mu\ip{g_\mu}\theta$, and for $\alpha>0$ let $\kappa^*(\alpha)$ solve $\alpha_c(\kappa^*)=\alpha$. If $n,d\to\infty$ with $n/d\to\alpha$, then $\Prob(\kappa_{\max}\ge\kappa^*(\alpha)+\eps)\to0$ for every $\eps>0$; and if $\alpha<2$, then $\kappa_{\max}\to\kappa^*(\alpha)>0$ in probability.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The function $\varrho$ is continuous and strictly increasing, with $\varrho'(\kappa)=2\E(Z+\kappa)_+>0$ and limits $0$ and $\infty$, so $\kappa^*$ is well defined and $\alpha_c(\kappa^*+\eps)<\alpha<\alpha_c(\kappa^*-\eps)$. The maximum defining $\kappa_{\max}$ is attained, so $\{\kappa_{\max}\ge\kappa\}=\cF_\kappa$. \Cref{thm:gordon:capacity}(i) at $\kappa^*+\eps$ gives the first claim. If $\alpha<2$, then $\kappa^*>0$, and for $\eps<\kappa^*$ part (ii) at $\kappa^*-\eps$ gives $\Prob(\kappa_{\max}<\kappa^*-\eps)\to0$.
\end{proof}

Gardner's maximal stability is therefore the largest margin of random labels at every load below $2$; and Gardner's $q\to1$ is the auxiliary norm reaching the boundary of the ball (\cref{ex:gordon:norm}).

\begin{remark}[what the comparison does not see]\label{rem:gordon:volume}
\Textcite[Theorem~3]{ShcherbinaTirozzi2003} proved, for $\pm1$ patterns, that for $\kappa\ge0$ and $\alpha\le\alpha_c(\kappa)$ the truncated log-volume is self-averaging and converges to $\min_q\phi_{\mathrm{RS}}(q)$, Gardner's whole free entropy; \textcite[Chapter~8]{Talagrand2011b} also derives the Gardner formula for the sphere. Both work with the volume, a partition function at positive temperature, which the min--max argument never meets. Part (i) does turn the ground state \eqref{eq:gordon:ground} into a lower bound, $\xi_S/\sqrt d\ge(\sqrt{\alpha\varrho}-1)_+-\eps$ with probability tending to one (\cref{ex:gordon:groundstate}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Beyond convexity: negative margins and binary weights}\label{sec:gordon:beyond}

For $\kappa<0$ the ball contains the feasible point $\theta=0$, and $-1/\kappa_{\max}$ is the largest norm in the polytope $\{G\theta\ge-\mathbf 1\}$ \parencite{MontanariZhongZhou2024}. Part~(i) of \cref{thm:gordon:capacity} still gives Gardner's value as an upper bound, which, as \textcite[Section~2.3]{Stojnic2013a} reports, answers a conjecture of Talagrand (his Conjecture~8.4.4, which we have not seen). \Textcite{Stojnic2013b} improved it by comparing an exponential of the cost, and \textcite[Definition~2, Theorem~4.2]{MontanariZhongZhou2024} proved the resulting \term{lifted bound}\index{lifted Gordon bound}: with $\ell_\kappa(u)=-\log\E e^{-u(\kappa-Z)_+^2}$, a load $\alpha$ exceeds the capacity if some $s>0$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gordon:lifted}
\frac1{\sqrt{s^2+4}+s}+\frac1s\log\frac{\sqrt{s^2+4}+s}2<\inf_{t>0}\Bigl\{\frac1{4t}+\frac\alpha s\,\ell_\kappa(st)\Bigr\}.
\end{equation}
As $s\to0$ this becomes Gardner's condition $\alpha\varrho(\kappa)>1$. Evaluated numerically, \eqref{eq:gordon:lifted} reproduces the tables of \textcite{Stojnic2013b} ($12.784$ against $13.273$ at $\kappa=-1$), which show no improvement at $\kappa=-0.5$ and $-0.6$ and one of $1.3\times10^{-4}$ at $-0.63$; Stojnic presents them as an illustration and states no threshold. The bound improves on Gardner's value exactly for $\kappa<\kappa_\times=-0.6219$. A second-order expansion in $s$ (\cref{ex:gordon:lifted}) shows that at $\alpha=\alpha_c(\kappa)$ small $s$ succeed exactly when $\Var[(Z+\kappa)_+^2]<2\,\E[(Z+\kappa)_+^2]$, which holds exactly for $\kappa<\kappa_\times$ (the difference changes sign once on $(-\infty,0)$); for $\kappa_\times<\kappa<0$ we found no $s$ that succeeds below $\alpha_c$ (margins every $0.02$, values of $s$ from $10^{-4}$ to $30$). \Textcite{MontanariZhongZhou2024} also proved that the capacity is $(1+o(1))\log\abs\kappa/\Phi(\kappa)$ as $\kappa\to-\infty$, while Gardner's value is $(1+o(1))\kappa^2/(2\Phi(\kappa))$, and that their linear program, which maximizes $\ip v\theta$ over $\{\theta:G\theta\ge\kappa\mathbf 1,\ \norm\theta\le1\}$ for a random direction $v$, returns a unit vector with high probability exactly when $\alpha<1/\Phi(\kappa)$ (their Theorem~4.3).

\paragraph{Binary weights.} For $w\in\{\pm1\}^d$ a conditional first moment, outlined by \textcite[Section~5]{KrauthMezard1989} and completed by \textcite{AltschulerTikhomirov2024}, beats the first-moment bound $1$. A binary solution is a point of the spherical solution set at $\kappa=0$, and a random rotation makes it uniform; so, when the spherical log-volume is near its typical value $d\,\mathrm{GD}(\alpha)$, with $\mathrm{GD}(\alpha)=\min_q\phi_{\mathrm{RS}}(q)$ at $\kappa=0$, there are at most $2^de^{d(\mathrm{GD}(\alpha)+\eps)}$ binary solutions on average, and the capacity is at most the root of $\mathrm{GD}(\alpha)=-\log2$, $0.846557$. At $q=\frac12$, where $\E\log H(U)=-1$ because $H(U)$ is uniform, $\phi_{\mathrm{RS}}(\frac12)=-\alpha+\frac12(1-\log2)$, so $\alpha_c\le(1+\log2)/2=0.846574$, a bound that needs no numerical evaluation. Krauth and Mézard read $\alpha\approx0.85$ and $q\approx0.5$ off Gardner's results, and Altschuler and Tikhomirov evaluate $\phi_{\mathrm{RS}}(\frac12)$ in this way at $\alpha=0.847$; neither writes the closed form, which we record as the book's observation. Their Proposition~1 states $\mathrm{GD}(0.847)<-\log2-0.002$, but the value at $q=\frac12$ is $-\log2-0.00043$ and the minimum over $q$ is $-\log2-0.00045$; the margin should read $0.0004$, which leaves their bound $0.847$ intact, since their argument needs only a positive margin.

The value $\alpha_\star=0.8330786$ of Krauth and Mézard is proved under numerical conditions: \textcite{DingSun2019} proved the lower bound with positive probability under a one-variable condition, sharp-threshold results \parencite{Xu2021,NakajimaSun2023} upgrade it to high probability, and \textcite{Huang2024} proved the upper bound under a two-variable condition. \Textcite{Shmalo2026} verified both conditions by a computer-assisted proof (arXiv version~1, July 2026); his paper states that the proof has not yet been independently reproduced or peer reviewed, and its declarations say: ``Most of the work reported here was done by two AI systems'' (Fable~5 of Anthropic and Codex of OpenAI). For the symmetric binary perceptron, with constraints $\abs{\ip{g_\mu}w}/\sqrt d\le\kappa$, the capacity is the first-moment value $-\log2/\log\Prob(\abs Z\le\kappa)$, derived by \textcite{AubinPerkinsZdeborova2019} and proved by \textcite{AbbeLiSly2021}.

\section{The status board}\label{sec:gordon:status}

For nonnegative margins the capacity is settled, with three proofs. For negative margins Gardner's formula is a proved upper bound, known to fail for sufficiently negative margins and, according to physics, for every negative margin; the capacity is open. For binary weights the replica-symmetric criterion is wrong, and the Krauth--Mézard value is proved under numerical conditions that a 2026 preprint verifies by computer.

\section{Where the translation fails}\label{sec:gordon:fails}

\paragraph{What was assumed.} Gaussian patterns with random labels: \textcite{ShcherbinaTirozzi2003} treat $\pm1$ patterns, but the CGMT needs a Gaussian matrix, and a teacher changes the question (\cref{ch:classification}). The limit is proportional, and the bounds of \cref{thm:gordon:capacity} locate the transition to $O(\sqrt d)$ patterns without describing its shape. The theorem concerns feasibility, not the volume, and its convex half rests on relaxing the sphere to the ball, which is exact only because $\kappa>0$ lets a solution be rescaled onto the sphere.

\paragraph{Where the physics is wrong, or not yet right.} The replica-symmetric capacity is wrong for $\kappa<0$, as the physics itself says: the replicon exceeds $1$ at capacity, and \textcite{FranzEtAl2017} place the jamming line in a phase with full replica-symmetry breaking. The rigorous confirmation is partial: the lifted bound proves the failure for $\kappa<-0.6219$, by an expansion and the numerical root of an explicit function, and \textcite{MontanariZhongZhou2024} prove that Gardner's value is too large by a factor of order $\kappa^2/\log\abs\kappa$ as $\kappa\to-\infty$; between $-0.622$ and $0$ nobody has proved it wrong. The caption of their Figure~1 calls their upper bound strictly better than Gardner's for all negative $\kappa$; our evaluation of their Definition~2 finds no improvement for $\kappa>-0.622$, in agreement with Stojnic's tables. For binary weights the criterion $q\to1$ gives the impossible $4/\pi$.

\paragraph{Open problems.} Prove that the replica-symmetric value fails for every $\kappa<0$, in particular for $-0.622<\kappa<0$, and identify the capacity that full replica-symmetry breaking predicts \parencite{FranzEtAl2017}; a comparison inequality that is tight beyond convexity would do both. \Textcite{MontanariZhongZhou2024} raise the question of how algorithms other than their linear program behave; \cref{fig:gordon:margin}(b) suggests that simple heuristics come within a few hundredths of Gardner's value at moderate loads. For binary weights, an independent check or a conceptual proof of the conditions behind $\alpha_\star$ remains to be given.

\begin{exercises}
\item \consolidation (The function $\varrho$.) Show that $\varrho'(\kappa)=2(\kappa\Phi(\kappa)+\phi(\kappa))$ and $\varrho''(\kappa)=2\Phi(\kappa)$; that $\alpha_c(\kappa)(1+\kappa^2)\to1$ as $\kappa\to\infty$ and $2\Phi(\kappa)\alpha_c(\kappa)/\kappa^2\to1$ as $\kappa\to-\infty$; and check $\alpha_c(1)=0.5196$ and $\alpha_c(2)=0.2002$.\label{ex:gordon:rho}

\item \consolidation (The smallest singular value.) For $n\ge d$, write $\sigma_{\min}(G)=\min_{\theta\in S^{d-1}}\max_{\norm u\le1}u^\top G\theta$, show that its auxiliary value is $(\norm g-\norm h)_+$, and deduce $\Prob(\sigma_{\min}(G)<\sqrt{n-1}-\sqrt d-t)\le4/t^2$ for $0<t<\sqrt{n-1}-\sqrt d$.\label{ex:gordon:smin}

\item \consolidation (Cover's formula.) (a) Show that almost surely some unit vector has $G\theta\ge0$ if and only if some has $G\theta>0$. \hint{almost surely every $d$ rows of $G$ are linearly independent; perturb a weak solution along a vector with inner product $1$ with the rows where it is tight.} (b) Let $n^*$ be the largest $n$ for which the first $n$ patterns of an infinite sequence are separable. Show that $\E n^*=2d$. \hint{$\Prob(n^*\ge n)=\Prob(\mathrm{Bin}(n-1,\frac12)\le d-1)$; count fair coin flips until the $d$th head.}\label{ex:gordon:cover}

\item \byhand (Near capacity.) Keep the logarithmic term of $\log H$ in \cref{comp:gordon:capacity} to show that $\phi_{\mathrm{RS}}=\frac{1-\alpha\varrho}{2\eps}+\frac{1+\alpha\Phi(\kappa)}2\log\eps+O(1)$ with $\eps=1-q$, hence $1-q^*\simeq(1-\alpha/\alpha_c)/(1+\alpha\Phi(\kappa))$.\label{ex:gordon:near}

\item \byhand (Binary weights.) Show that $q\to1$ in the stationarity equations of \eqref{eq:gordon:isingRS} forces $\alpha=2/(\pi\varrho(\kappa))$. The first moment bounds the capacity by $\log2/(-\log H(\kappa))$; show that the replica-symmetric value exceeds it at $\kappa=0.5$ ($0.612$ against $0.589$) but not at $\kappa=1$ ($0.331$ against $0.377$).\label{ex:gordon:ising}

\item \byhand\hard (Where the lifted bound starts.) Let $X=(Z+\kappa)_+^2$ have mean $\varrho$ and variance $v$. Show that the left side of \eqref{eq:gordon:lifted} is $1-\frac s4+O(s^2)$ and the right side $\sqrt{\alpha\varrho}-\frac{sv}{8\varrho}+O(s^2)$, so that at $\alpha=\alpha_c(\kappa)$ small $s$ succeed when $v<2\varrho$; locate $\kappa_\times=-0.6219$ numerically. \hint{$\ell_\kappa(u)=u\varrho-\frac{u^2}2v+O(u^3)$; evaluate the correction at $t_0=1/(2\sqrt{\alpha\varrho})$.}\label{ex:gordon:lifted}

\item \translate (The heat-equation step.) For $0<q<1$ let $\cZ_q(h)=H((\kappa-h)/\sqrt{1-q})$. Prove with \cref{thm:toolkit:gaussianibp} that
\[
\frac{\dd}{\dd q}\E\log\cZ_q(\sqrt qZ)=-\frac12\E\bigl[(\partial_h\log\cZ_q)(\sqrt qZ)^2\bigr],
\]
and derive \eqref{eq:gordon:saddle}. Which step of the replica computation does this justify, and which not?\label{ex:gordon:heat}

\item \translate (The auxiliary norm.) For $\kappa>0$, show that the minimum-norm solution of $G\theta\ge\kappa\mathbf 1$ has norm $\kappa/\kappa_{\max}$, that $\cA_B=\min_{0\le t\le1}(\norm{(tg+\kappa\mathbf 1)_+}-t\norm h)_+$, and that the smallest $t$ at which this vanishes tends to $\kappa/\kappa^*(\alpha)$. Translate Gardner's $q\to1$.\label{ex:gordon:norm}

\item \translate\hard (The ground state as a theorem.) Show that $\xi_S/\sqrt d\ge(\sqrt{\alpha\varrho(\kappa)}-1)_+-\eps$ with probability tending to one, for every $\kappa$ and $\eps>0$. Why would a matching upper bound need part (ii) of the CGMT, and why does the ball relaxation fail for it when $\alpha>\varrho(\kappa)/\Phi(\kappa)^2$, even for $\kappa>0$? \hint{differentiate $t(\norm{(g+\kappa\mathbf 1/t)_+}-\norm h)$ at $t=1$; $\E[Z(Z+\kappa)_+]=\Phi(\kappa)$.}\label{ex:gordon:groundstate}

\item \measurement (The auxiliary window.) Show that $\Var\norm{g_+}\to\frac58$ and $\Var\norm h\to\frac12$ at $n=2d$, so that $\Prob(D_0\le0)\approx\Phi(-(\alpha-2)\sqrt d/4.24)$, while Cover's probability is about $\Phi(-(\alpha-2)\sqrt d/\sqrt2)$; check both against \texttt{08-gordon-\allowbreak cover.py} at $d=100$. Then edit the sizes in \texttt{08-gordon-\allowbreak negative.py} to run it at $d=50$, $100$, $200$: does the gap between the soft-minimum algorithm and Gardner's curve shrink?\label{ex:gordon:window}

\item \research (Between $-0.622$ and $0$.) Prove that the capacity is strictly below $\alpha_c(\kappa)$ for some $\kappa\in(-0.622,0)$.
