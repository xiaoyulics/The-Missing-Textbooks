\begin{chaptersummary}
Maximum likelihood for tensor PCA minimizes a random polynomial on the sphere whose noise part is the pure spherical $p$-spin glass. Physicists count its critical points as TAP states, averaging the Kac--Rice integral with a self-averaging Hessian determinant, and find exponentially many local minima between the ground state $-E_0$ and a threshold $-\kacriceinf$, where minima become marginal and descent is predicted to stop. We prove the mean counts exactly at every size, by a Gaussian regression and an identity that turns the absolute determinant of a GOE matrix into the density of one more eigenvalue, and in the limit by large deviations of the extreme eigenvalue; that the typical count equals the mean below the threshold is Subag's second-moment theorem. Exhaustive enumeration at small $N$ matches the exact formula; gradient descent at $N\le200$ stops above $-\kacriceinf$, where the exact formula places almost all minima at these sizes; warm-started descent on the spiked tensor lands on the predicted overlap. As of 2026 the typical landscape near the spike and the endpoint of gradient descent are not proved.
\end{chaptersummary}

\section{The question: how rugged is the likelihood of tensor PCA?}\label{sec:kacrice:question}

In \term{tensor PCA}\index{tensor PCA} \parencite{MontanariRichard2014} one observes the $p$-tensor $Y=\lambda N^{-(p-1)/2}(x^*)^{\otimes p}+G$, where $x^*$ lies on the sphere $S_N=\{\sigma\in\R^N:\norm\sigma^2=N\}$, $G$ has independent $\Normal(0,1)$ entries, and $\lambda\ge0$ is the signal-to-noise ratio. On $S_N$ the log-likelihood of $\sigma$ is an increasing affine function of $\ip{Y}{\sigma^{\otimes p}}$, so the \term{maximum likelihood estimator}\index{maximum likelihood estimator!tensor PCA} minimizes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kacrice:spiked}
H_{N,\lambda}(\sigma)=H_N(\sigma)-\lambda N\,m(\sigma)^p,\qquad m(\sigma)=\frac{\ip{\sigma}{x^*}}N,
\end{equation}
where, with $J=-G$,
$H_N(\sigma)=N^{-(p-1)/2}\sum_{i_1,\dots,i_p=1}^NJ_{i_1\cdots i_p}\sigma_{i_1}\cdots\sigma_{i_p}$. The noise part $H_N$ is the \term{pure spherical $p$-spin}\index{spherical p-spin@spherical $p$-spin model} glass in the normalization of \textcite{AuffingerBenArousCerny2013} that this book uses: a centered Gaussian process with $\E H_N(\sigma)H_N(\sigma')=N\,R(\sigma,\sigma')^p$, $R(\sigma,\sigma')=\ip\sigma{\sigma'}/N$. The case $\lambda=0$ is the pure model; energies are per spin.

For $p\ge3$ the estimator is hard to compute but statistically strong: it correlates with $x^*$ once $\lambda$ exceeds a constant \parencite{JagannathLopattoMiolane2020}. Efficient methods need far more: gradient flow and Langevin dynamics from a uniform start with $m>0$ reach the spike in time of order one when $\lambda=N^\alpha$, $\alpha>(p-2)/2$ \parencite[Theorem~2.1]{BenArousGheissariJagannath2020}, and sum-of-squares relaxations succeed, for odd $p$, from $\lambda$ of order $N^{(p-2)/4}$ up to a logarithm \parencite{HopkinsShiSteurer2015}. Why does descent fail for a range of $\lambda$ growing with $N$, when the minimizer is good? The physicist's answer: the landscape is rugged, with exponentially many local minima, and descent stops in one of them.

A \term{critical point}\index{critical point} of a smooth function on $S_N$ is a zero of its gradient along the sphere; its \term{index}\index{critical point!index} is the number of negative eigenvalues of its Hessian along the sphere (local minima have index $0$). For Borel $B\subset\R$, $\kacriceCrt_N(B)$ counts the critical points of $H_N$ with $H_N(\sigma)/N\in B$, and $\kacriceCrt_{N,k}(B)$ those of index $k$. The \term{annealed complexity}\index{complexity!annealed} is $\frac1N\log\E\kacriceCrt_N(B)$; the \term{quenched complexity}\index{complexity!quenched} $\frac1N\E\log\kacriceCrt_N(B)$ describes a typical landscape.\sidenote{Physicists call the logarithm of the number of states per degree of freedom the complexity, or configurational entropy, of a glass. The word has nothing to do with computational complexity, though this chapter is about a link between the two.}

\begin{physicsbox}[Crisanti and Sommers 1995; Kurchan and Laloux 1996; Cugliandolo and Kurchan 1993; Ros et al.\ 2019]
Let $N\to\infty$, $\kacriceinf=2\sqrt{(p-1)/p}$, and $J(x)=\frac x4\sqrt{x^2-4}-\log\frac{x+\sqrt{x^2-4}}2$ for $x\ge2$, $J=0$ on $[0,2]$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item In the pure model the number of critical points with energy near $u$ is, on average and typically, $e^{N\Sigma(u)}$ with
\[
\Sigma(u)=\tfrac12\log(p-1)-\tfrac{p-2}{4(p-1)}u^2-J\bigl(2\abs u/\kacriceinf\bigr).
\]
\item Below the \term{threshold energy}\index{threshold energy} $-\kacriceinf$ almost all critical points are local minima; above it they are saddles of index proportional to $N$. Minima fill $[-E_0,-\kacriceinf]$, where $-E_0$, the zero of $\Sigma$, is the ground state; the most numerous sit at $-\kacriceinf$ and are \term{marginal}\index{marginal stability}, with Hessian eigenvalues down to $0$. Saddles of index $k$ appear only above $-E_k$, where $E_0>E_1>\dots\to\kacriceinf$. For $p=3$, $E_0=1.65700$ and $\kacriceinf=1.63299$.
\item Gradient descent from a random start ends at $-\kacriceinf$, not at the ground state.
\item In the spiked model the threshold minima near the equator $m=0$ survive at every fixed $\lambda$. A local minimum correlated with $x^*$ appears at $\lambda_c(p)$ and becomes the global minimum at $\kacriceML(p)>\lambda_c(p)$; for $p=3$, $\lambda_c=2/\sqrt3$ and $\kacriceML\approx1.21$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{physicsbox}

Parts~(i) and~(ii) are the TAP complexity of \textcite{CrisantiSommers1995} with the index layers of \textcite{KurchanLaloux1996}, (iii) the dynamical prediction of \textcite{CugliandoloKurchan1993}, (iv) the replicated Kac--Rice computation of \textcite{RosEtAl2019} in our normalization. How do physicists derive them, and what is proved? We prove the mean counts of~(i) and~(ii) exactly at every $N$ and in the limit; the typical counts are Subag's theorem; (iii) is a conjecture whose Langevin version \cref{ch:dmft} discusses; (iv) is proved for mean counts and for the estimator, not for the typical landscape.

\section{The physics: counting TAP states}\label{sec:kacrice:physics}

\subsection{States are critical points}\label{sec:kacrice:tap}

At low temperature a physicist describes a spin glass by its \term{states}, the local minima of the \term{TAP free energy}\index{TAP free energy} of the local magnetizations (\cref{ch:amp}). For the spherical $p$-spin, \textcite{KurchanParisiVirasoro1993} and \textcite{CrisantiSommers1995} wrote it for magnetizations $\sqrt q\,\sigma$, $\sigma\in S_N$, $0\le q<1$; per spin, in our normalization and with the physicists' inverse temperature $\beta$ \parencite[eq.~(6.3)]{AuffingerBenArousCerny2013},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kacrice:tap}
f_{\mathrm{TAP}}(q,\sigma)=\frac{q^{p/2}H_N(\sigma)}{\sqrt2\,N}-\frac{\log(1-q)}{2\beta}-\frac\beta4\bigl(1+(p-1)q^p-pq^{p-1}\bigr).
\end{equation}
The direction enters only through $H_N(\sigma)/N$, so at a stationary point $\sigma$ is a critical point of $H_N$ and $q$ solves a scalar equation in that energy, with exactly two solutions below a temperature-dependent energy \parencite[Section~6]{AuffingerBenArousCerny2013}: counting TAP states is counting critical points, and at zero temperature the states are the local minima of $H_N$.\sidenote{In \eqref{eq:kacrice:tap} the first term is the energy of the magnetization, the second is minus the temperature times the entropy $\frac12\log(1-q)$ of the fluctuations around it, and the third is the Onsager reaction term.}

\subsection{The annealed count}\label{sec:kacrice:annealed-physics}

\begin{heuristic}[The count by a delta function]
Write the number of critical points with energy in $[u,u+\dd u]$ as $\int_{S_N}\delta(\kacricegrad H_N)\abs{\det\kacriceHess H_N}\,\delta(H_N/N-u)\dd\sigma$ and average over the couplings. The law of $H_N$ is rotation invariant, so the count is the area $\abs{S_N}$ times a one-point quantity. At a point, the covariance $N^{1-p}\ip\sigma{\sigma'}^p$ gives the joint law of the value (variance $N$), the tangential gradient (independent coordinates of variance $p$) and the tangential second derivatives $G$, with $\E G_{ij}G_{kl}=\frac{p(p-1)}N(\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}+\delta_{il}\delta_{jk})$; the three are uncorrelated. The sphere adds the Lagrange multiplier $pH_N/N$ on the diagonal, so at energy $u$ the Hessian is
\[
\sqrt{p(p-1)}\,\bigl(A-t(u)I\bigr),\qquad t(u)=u\sqrt{\tfrac p{p-1}}=\frac{2u}{\kacriceinf},
\]
with $A$ a GOE matrix of size $N-1$, semicircle on $[-2,2]$. The gradient has density $(2\pi p)^{-(N-1)/2}$ at $0$, the energy $\sqrt{N/(2\pi)}\,e^{-Nu^2/2}$ at $u$. Now treat the spectrum of $A$ as self-averaging, $\E\abs{\det(A-t)}\approx e^{N\Phi(t)}$ with $\Phi(t)=\int\log\abs{y-t}\,\sigma_{\mathrm{sc}}(\dd y)$, and turn the prefactor $\abs{S_N}(2\pi p)^{-(N-1)/2}(p(p-1))^{(N-1)/2}$ into $e^{\frac N2(1+\log(p-1))+o(N)}$ by Stirling's formula. The complexity at energy $u$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kacrice:sigma}
\Sigma(u)=\tfrac12\bigl(1+\log(p-1)\bigr)-\tfrac12u^2+\Phi\bigl(t(u)\bigr).\qedhere
\end{equation}
\end{heuristic}

\begin{computation}{The complexity from the semicircle}\label{comp:kacrice:potential}
\step \emph{Inside the bulk.} The Stieltjes transform $s(z)=\frac12(-z+\sqrt{z^2-4})$ of the semicircle (\cref{thm:toolkit:semicircle}) has real part $-t/2$ at $z=t+\mathrm i0$, $\abs t<2$, so $\Phi'(t)=-\mathrm{Re}\,s(t+\mathrm i0)=t/2$. With $\Phi(0)=\int\log\abs y\,\sigma_{\mathrm{sc}}(\dd y)=-\frac12$ (substitute $y=2\cos\theta$), $\Phi(t)=\frac{t^2}4-\frac12$ for $\abs t\le2$.
\step \emph{Outside.} For $t>2$, $\Phi'(t)=-s(t)=\frac12(t-\sqrt{t^2-4})$, so $\Phi(t)=\frac{t^2}4-\frac12-J(t)$, with $J(t)=\frac12\int_2^t\sqrt{y^2-4}\dd y$ the rate function of the largest GOE eigenvalue (\cref{rem:toolkit:goeldp}); $\Phi$ is even.
\step \emph{The complexity.} Since $t(u)^2=\frac p{p-1}u^2$, \eqref{eq:kacrice:sigma} is the formula of the box. For $p=3$, $\Sigma(0)=\frac12\log2=0.34657$, $\Sigma(-\kacriceinf)=\frac12\log2-\frac13=0.013240$, and $\Sigma(u)=0$ at $u=-1.656998=-E_0$.
\step \emph{The index.} At energy $u$ the Hessian spectrum fills $\sqrt{p(p-1)}\,[-2-t,2-t]$ with semicircle density; a fraction $\sigma_{\mathrm{sc}}((-2,t))$ of it is negative. For $u<-\kacriceinf$ the typical critical point is a local minimum; at $u=-\kacriceinf$ the spectrum touches $0$.
\end{computation}

Below the threshold the complexity of minima is $\Sigma_0(u)=\Sigma(u)$; above it, minima would need the whole semicircle on the positive side, and physicists conclude that there are none on the exponential scale. A saddle of index $k$ below the threshold needs $k$ eigenvalues of $A$ below $t$, outside the semicircle, each at the cost $J(\abs t)$: its complexity $\Sigma(u)-kJ(\abs{t(u)})$ vanishes at $-E_k$ \parencite{KurchanLaloux1996}. Since $\Sigma_0$ increases up to $-\kacriceinf$, the most numerous minima sit at the threshold, where the lowest Hessian eigenvalues touch zero: they are marginal. That is the landscape behind part~(iii): descent slows down among marginal minima and stops where they are typical (\cref{fig:kacrice:complexity}).

\subsection{The typical count and the ground state}\label{sec:kacrice:replicas}

The mean count can be dominated by rare landscapes. Physicists compute the typical count with replicas: $\E\log\kacriceCrt=\lim_{n\to0}(\E\kacriceCrt^n-1)/n$, where $\E\kacriceCrt^n$ counts $n$-tuples of critical points interacting through their overlaps $q_{ab}$. If the dominant tuples are orthogonal ($q_{ab}=0$ for $a\ne b$), then $\E\kacriceCrt^n\approx(\E\kacriceCrt)^n$ and the quenched complexity equals the annealed one, as physicists expect for the pure model below the threshold \parencite{CrisantiSommers1995}; the ground state is then the zero of $\Sigma_0$. A second derivation agrees: the zero-temperature limit of the one-step replica-symmetry-breaking solution of \textcite{CrisantiSommers1992} gives the ground state $-\inf_{c,d>0}\frac12[c+pd+\frac1c\log(1+\frac cd)]$ \parencite[eq.~(5.11)]{AuffingerBenArousCerny2013}; for $p=3$ the infimum, at $c=0.62502$, $d=0.34399$, is $1.6569983635$, the zero of $\Sigma_0$ to ten digits.\sidenote{Some computations drop the absolute value of the determinant. Each critical point then counts with the sign $(-1)^{\text{index}}$, and the count over all energies is the Euler characteristic of the sphere (\cref{ex:kacrice:euler}); below the threshold, where almost all critical points are minima, the two counts agree on the exponential scale. \Textcite{CavagnaGiardinaParisi1998} counted stationary points of every index of the TAP free energy.}

\subsection{The spike}\label{sec:kacrice:spike-physics}

With the spike, the count changes in three ways at a point of overlap $m$. The energy shifts by $-\lambda m^p$; the gradient shifts by $\lambda pm^{p-1}$ times the tangential part of $x^*$, which costs $e^{-Np\lambda^2m^{2p-2}(1-m^2)/2}$ in the density of the gradient at $0$; and the Hessian acquires the rank-one term $-\sqrt{p(p-1)}\,\theta(m)ee^\top$ along the tangential direction $e$ of $x^*$, $\theta(m)=\lambda\sqrt{p(p-1)}\,m^{p-2}(1-m^2)$, which for $\theta>1$ pulls an eigenvalue of $A-\theta ee^\top$ out of the semicircle to $-(\theta+1/\theta)$ (the BBP transition of \cref{ch:spiked}) and can turn a would-be minimum into a saddle. With the entropy $\frac12\log(1-m^2)$ of the points at overlap $m$, the complexity at overlap $m$ and energy $u$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kacrice:sigmalambda}
\begin{split}
\Sigma_\lambda(m,u)={}&\tfrac12\bigl(1+\log(p-1)\bigr)+\tfrac12\log(1-m^2)-\tfrac p2\lambda^2m^{2p-2}(1-m^2)\\
&-\tfrac12(u+\lambda m^p)^2+\Phi\bigl(t(u)\bigr).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
For $p\ge3$ the spike has no gradient and no curvature at the equator, where $\Sigma_\lambda(0,u)=\Sigma(u)$: the threshold minima survive there at every $\lambda$. Away from it, $\sup_u\Sigma_\lambda(m,u)$ can touch zero again at an overlap $m^*$ near $1$, an isolated minimum correlated with the truth, exactly when $m^{2p-4}(1-m^2)=1/(p\lambda^2)$ has a root, that is for
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kacrice:lambdac}
\lambda\ge\lambda_c(p)=\Bigl(\frac{(p-1)^{p-1}}{p\,(p-2)^{p-2}}\Bigr)^{1/2},
\end{equation}
$\lambda_c(3)=2/\sqrt3=1.15470$; $m^*$ is the largest root. \Textcite{RosEtAl2019} computed the typical complexity by a replica-symmetric replicated Kac--Rice formula. For $p=3$ a band of minima around the equator grows with $\lambda$; at $\lambda_c$ an isolated minimum detaches from its top, at $\kacriceML\approx1.21$ it becomes the global minimum, and the band shrinks but never disappears. The two thresholds are the inverse dynamical and static temperatures of the pure model, $T_d=\sqrt3/2$ and $T_s=0.8288$ in our normalization (\cref{ch:dmft}).\sidenote{\Textcite[eq.~(58)]{RosEtAl2019} write $r_c=\frac p2\beta_d$ and $r_{\mathrm{1ST}}=\frac p2\beta_s$; their spike is $rNm^k/k$ in physicists' energies, so $r=p\lambda/\sqrt2$.}

The derivations contain five uncontrolled steps: the annealed average, where the typical count is wanted; the replacement of $\E\abs{\det}$ by the exponential of the mean logarithm, which exchanges an expectation with the exponential of an extensive quantity; the continuation $n\to0$; the zero-overlap ansatz; and the identification of where descent stops with where minima are most numerous.

\section{The stone}\label{sec:kacrice:stone}

\begin{stone}{the TAP count of Crisanti and Sommers, with replicas}{Auffinger, Ben Arous and \v{C}ern\'y; Subag; Ben Arous, Mei, Montanari and Nica}
\stonestep{Count}{Count states by $\int\delta(\nabla H)\abs{\det\nabla^2H}\,\delta(H-Nu)$ over the sphere, averaged over the couplings.}{The Kac--Rice formula (\cref{thm:toolkit:kacrice}), exact under nondegeneracy and a modulus of continuity.}
\stonestep{The Hessian}{By isotropy the Hessian is a GOE matrix shifted by $p$ times the energy, independent of the gradient.}{\Cref{lem:kacrice:regression}: value, gradient and second derivatives at a point are independent Gaussians, exactly at every $N$.}
\stonestep{The determinant}{Self-averaging: $\E\abs{\det(A-t)}\approx e^{N\Phi(t)}$, $\Phi$ the logarithmic potential of the semicircle.}{\Cref{lem:kacrice:extra}: $\E\abs{\det(A-t)}$ is $c_Ne^{Nt^2/4}$ times the density of an eigenvalue of a $\kacriceGOE(N)$ matrix at $t$, exactly. No physical counterpart; the constant comes from $p=2$ (\cref{lem:kacrice:two}).}
\stonestep{The saddle point}{Maximize the exponent over the energy.}{The semicircle law inside the bulk, the large deviations of the extreme eigenvalue outside it (\cref{thm:kacrice:annealed}).}
\stonestep{The threshold}{Minima need the shifted semicircle on the positive side, $u\le-\kacriceinf$; at $-\kacriceinf$ they are marginal.}{The rate of the smallest eigenvalue is infinite inside the bulk: minima above $-\kacriceinf$ are rarer in mean than every exponential (\cref{cor:kacrice:groundstate}).}
\stonestep{The typical count}{Replicas with zero mutual overlap: $\E\log\kacriceCrt=\log\E\kacriceCrt$.}{Subag's second moment (\cref{thm:kacrice:subag}): two replicas suffice, and nothing is continued in $n$.}
\stonestep{The ground state}{The zero of the complexity of minima, confirmed by the zero-temperature 1RSB free energy.}{The first moment gives $\liminf\kacriceGS_N\ge-E_0$ (\cref{cor:kacrice:groundstate}); the matching bound needs the second moment (Subag) or the Parisi formula (ABC, even $p$).}
\stonestep{The spike}{Shifted energy and gradient and a rank-one Hessian term whose BBP outlier destabilizes minima; replicated Kac--Rice for the typical landscape.}{The same regression (\cref{prop:kacrice:spiked}) and the outlier's large deviations give the mean landscape (\cref{thm:kacrice:bammn}), the Parisi formula the estimator (\cref{thm:kacrice:jlm}); the typical landscape has no counterpart yet.}
\end{stone}

The translation is closer here than in most chapters: the physicist's integral is the Kac--Rice formula, and the covariance computation behind the shifted GOE is the same on both sides. The proof replaces two steps. Self-averaging becomes an exact identity at every $N$, stronger than needed; the saddle point over the energy becomes a large-deviation estimate for one eigenvalue, the only place where the proof needs more than the semicircle law, and \enquote{no minima above the threshold} becomes the infinite rate of that eigenvalue inside the bulk. The routes part at the typical count: the replicas are continued in their number and assumed orthogonal, the proof computes exactly two, which is all concentration needs; for mixed models the assumption fails (\cref{sec:kacrice:fails}).

\section{The proof: the mean number of critical points}\label{sec:kacrice:proof}

We prove the mean counts of the pure model exactly at every $N$ (\cref{thm:kacrice:exact}; \textcite{AuffingerBenArousCerny2013}, Theorems~2.1--2.2) and their rates for all critical points and for minima (\cref{thm:kacrice:annealed}; their Theorems~2.8 and~2.5 with $k=0$), given the Kac--Rice formula, the GOE facts of \cref{ch:toolkit} and the joint density of GOE eigenvalues. We follow their proof with two changes of our own: the constant comes from the case $p=2$, which they use only as a check (their Remark~2.3), instead of Selberg's integral; and we treat only the cases that need the large deviations of the largest eigenvalue alone.

\paragraph{Setting.} Fix integers $p\ge2$ and $N\ge2$, and extend $H_N$ to a homogeneous polynomial of degree $p$ on $\R^N$, with covariance $N^{1-p}\ip{\sigma}{\sigma'}^p$. For smooth $F$ on $\R^N$ and $\sigma\in S_N$, the gradient of $F$ along the sphere is $P_\sigma\nabla F(\sigma)$, $P_\sigma=I-\sigma\sigma^\top/N$, and its Hessian along the sphere is the form on $T_\sigma=\sigma^\perp$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kacrice:hess}
\kacriceHess F(\sigma)=P_\sigma\nabla^2F(\sigma)P_\sigma-\frac{\ip{\sigma}{\nabla F(\sigma)}}N\,P_\sigma,
\end{equation}
the second derivative of $F$ along the great circle $s\mapsto\sigma\cos(s/\sqrt N)+\sqrt Nv\sin(s/\sqrt N)$, $v\perp\sigma$ a unit vector. For $H_N$, Euler's identity gives $\ip{\sigma}{\nabla H_N(\sigma)}=pH_N(\sigma)$. Let $W\sim\kacriceGOE(N)$ (\cref{def:toolkit:goe}), with ordered eigenvalues $\lambda_{(0)}\le\dots\le\lambda_{(N-1)}$ and empirical spectral measure $\mu_N$, and let $\rho_N$ be the density of $\E\mu_N$. Let $A$ be an $(N-1)\times(N-1)$ symmetric matrix with independent centered Gaussian entries $A_{ab}$, $a\le b$, of variances $(1+\delta_{ab})/N$, that is $A=((N-1)/N)^{1/2}W'$ with $W'\sim\kacriceGOE(N-1)$; its density is proportional to $e^{-N\tr(A^2)/4}$, and we write $A-t$ for $A-tI$. Finally $\kacriceinf=2\sqrt{(p-1)/p}$, $t(u)=2u/\kacriceinf$, $a=\frac{p-2}{4p}$, and $J$ is the function of the physics box.

\paragraph{The eigenvalue at the edge.} By \cref{rem:toolkit:goeldp}, $\lambda_{\max}(W)$ satisfies a large deviation principle with speed $N$ and a good rate function that is $+\infty$ on $(-\infty,2)$ and equal to $J$ on $[2,\infty)$, where $J$ is continuous and strictly increasing with $J(2)=0$. Applied to closed half-lines and open intervals, the principle gives, for $x\ge2$, $\delta>0$ and $y<2$,
\begin{gather}
\limsup_{N\to\infty}\tfrac1N\log\Prob\bigl(\lambda_{\max}(W)\ge x\bigr)\le-J(x),\label{eq:kacrice:ldp}\\
\liminf_{N\to\infty}\tfrac1N\log\Prob\bigl(x<\lambda_{\max}(W)<x+\delta\bigr)\ge-J(x),\notag\\
\lim_{N\to\infty}\tfrac1N\log\Prob\bigl(\lambda_{\max}(W)\le y\bigr)=-\infty,\notag
\end{gather}
and the same for $-\lambda_{(0)}$, because $-W\sim\kacriceGOE(N)$.

\begin{lemma}[Gaussian regression at a point]\label{lem:kacrice:regression}
Fix $\sigma\in S_N$ and an orthonormal basis $e_1,\dots,e_{N-1}$ of $T_\sigma$. The value $H_N(\sigma)$, the gradient $g=(\ip{e_i}{\nabla H_N(\sigma)})_{i<N}$ and the matrix $G=(\ip{e_i}{\nabla^2H_N(\sigma)e_j})_{i,j<N}$ are independent, with $H_N(\sigma)\sim\Normal(0,N)$, $g\sim\Normal(0,pI_{N-1})$, and $G$ distributed as $\sqrt{p(p-1)}\,A$. In the basis $(e_i)$, $\kacriceHess H_N(\sigma)=\sqrt{p(p-1)}\,(A-t(u)I)$ with $u=H_N(\sigma)/N$ and $A$ independent of $(u,g)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The plan is to compute the covariances at one point by differentiating the covariance function. For orthogonal $O$, $\sigma\mapsto H_N(O\sigma)$ has the covariance, hence the law, of $H_N$, so we may take $\sigma=\sqrt Ne_N$ and $e_i$ the standard basis vectors. Since $H_N$ is a polynomial with Gaussian coefficients, its derivatives are jointly Gaussian with $\E[\partial^\alpha H_N(x)\,\partial^\beta H_N(y)]=\partial_x^\alpha\partial_y^\beta C(x,y)$, $C(x,y)=N^{1-p}\ip xy^p$. At $x=y=\sigma$ we have $\ip\sigma\sigma=N$ and $\sigma_i=0$ for $i<N$, so every term with a factor $x_i$ or $y_i$, $i<N$, vanishes. For $i,j,k,l<N$ this leaves $\E H_N(\sigma)^2=N$, $\E[\partial_iH_N\,\partial_jH_N]=N^{1-p}pN^{p-1}\delta_{ij}=p\delta_{ij}$ and
\[
\E[\partial_{ij}H_N\,\partial_{kl}H_N]=\frac{p(p-1)}N(\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}+\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}),
\]
while every cross covariance keeps a factor $\sigma_i$ with $i<N$ and vanishes; for instance $\E[H_N\,\partial_iH_N]=pN^{1-p}\ip\sigma\sigma^{p-1}\sigma_i=0$. Jointly Gaussian and uncorrelated, the three blocks are independent, and $G$ has the covariance of $\sqrt{p(p-1)}A$. By \eqref{eq:kacrice:hess} and Euler's identity $\kacriceHess H_N(\sigma)=G-pu\,I$, and $pu=\sqrt{p(p-1)}\,t(u)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[one more eigenvalue]\label{lem:kacrice:extra}
There is a constant $c_N>0$ such that for every $k\in\{0,\dots,N-1\}$ and every measurable $\varphi\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kacrice:extra}
\int_\R\varphi(t)\,e^{-Nt^2/4}\,\E\bigl[\abs{\det(A-t)}\ind\{i(A-t)=k\}\bigr]\dd t=\frac{c_N}N\,\E\varphi\bigl(\lambda_{(k)}\bigr),
\end{equation}
with $i(M)$ the number of negative eigenvalues of $M$. Summed over $k$: $\int\varphi(t)e^{-Nt^2/4}\E\abs{\det(A-t)}\dd t=c_N\int\varphi\rho_N$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The plan is to recognize $\abs{\det(A-t)}$ as the missing factors of a Vandermonde determinant. By Theorem~2.5.2 of \textcite{AndersonGuionnetZeitouni2010}, rescaled, the eigenvalues of $W$ in a uniformly random order have the density $q_N(x)=Z_N^{-1}\abs{\Delta(x)}\prod_{i=1}^Ne^{-Nx_i^2/4}$ on $\R^N$, with $\Delta(x)=\prod_{i<j}(x_j-x_i)$, and those of $A$ have the density $Z'^{-1}_{N-1}\abs{\Delta(y)}\prod_ie^{-Ny_i^2/4}$ on $\R^{N-1}$. For eigenvalues $y_1,\dots,y_{N-1}$ of $A$, $\abs{\det(A-t)}=\prod_i\abs{y_i-t}$, the event $i(A-t)=k$ says that exactly $k$ of them lie below $t$, and $\abs{\Delta(y)}\prod_i\abs{y_i-t}=\abs{\Delta(y_1,\dots,y_{N-1},t)}$. By Tonelli's theorem the left side of \eqref{eq:kacrice:extra} is therefore $Z_N/Z'_{N-1}$ times $\E[\varphi(\lambda'_N)\ind\{\lambda'_N=\lambda_{(k)}\}]$, with $(\lambda'_1,\dots,\lambda'_N)$ the eigenvalues of $W$ in a uniformly random order, almost surely distinct. Given the set of eigenvalues, $\lambda'_N$ is each of them with probability $1/N$, so the expectation is $\E\varphi(\lambda_{(k)})/N$, and $c_N=Z_N/Z'_{N-1}$. For the sum, $\sum_k\E\varphi(\lambda_{(k)})=\E\sum_i\varphi(\lambda_i)=N\int\varphi\rho_N$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[two spins]\label{lem:kacrice:two}
For $p=2$, almost surely $H_N$ has exactly $2N$ critical points on $S_N$, all nondegenerate.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Here $H_N(\sigma)=\ip{\sigma}{B\sigma}$ with $B=(J+J^\top)/(2\sqrt N)=W/\sqrt2$ for a GOE matrix $W$. By \eqref{eq:kacrice:hess} the critical points are the eigenvectors of $B$ of norm $\sqrt N$. The eigenvalues of $W$ are almost surely distinct (\textcite{AndersonGuionnetZeitouni2010}, after Theorem~2.5.2), so the critical points are the $2N$ points $\pm\sqrt Nv_i$, and at $\sqrt Nv_i$ the Hessian $2(B-\mu_iI)$ on $v_i^\perp$ has the nonzero eigenvalues $2(\mu_j-\mu_i)$, $j\ne i$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[the exact mean count]\label{thm:kacrice:exact}
For all integers $p\ge2$, $N\ge2$ and $k\in\{0,\dots,N-1\}$ and every Borel set $B\subset\R$, with $t(B)=\{t(u):u\in B\}$,
\begin{gather}
\E\kacriceCrt_{N,k}(B)=2\sqrt{\tfrac2p}\,(p-1)^{N/2}\,\E\bigl[e^{-Na\lambda_{(k)}^2}\ind\{\lambda_{(k)}\in t(B)\}\bigr],\label{eq:kacrice:exact}\\
\E\kacriceCrt_N(B)=2N\sqrt{\tfrac2p}\,(p-1)^{N/2}\int_{t(B)}e^{-Nat^2}\rho_N(t)\dd t.\notag
\end{gather}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The plan is to apply the Kac--Rice formula, read the integrand off \cref{lem:kacrice:regression}, convert the determinant with \cref{lem:kacrice:extra}, and find the constant from \cref{lem:kacrice:two}.

\emph{The formula on $S_N$.} \Cref{thm:toolkit:kacrice} applies to $f(y)=H_N(\sqrt Ny)$ on the unit sphere. In the chart $y\mapsto(y_1,\dots,y_{N-1})$ near $e_N$, the first and second partial derivatives of $f$ at $e_N$ are the gradient and the Hessian of \cref{lem:kacrice:regression} (the term $\partial_Nf\cdot\partial_{ij}(1-\abs y^2)^{1/2}$ supplies $-pf\delta_{ij}$), jointly nondegenerate by the lemma, hence near $e_N$ by continuity; finitely many rotated charts cover the sphere. The chart derivatives are polynomials in $y$ and $(1-\abs y^2)^{1/2}$ with Gaussian coefficients, so hypothesis~(ii) holds with $\beta=1$, as \textcite{AuffingerBenArousCerny2013} verify. Under $\sigma=\sqrt Ny$ the factors $N^{N-1}$ (Hessian), $N^{-(N-1)/2}$ (density of the gradient) and $N^{-(N-1)/2}$ (surface measure) cancel: the formula holds on $S_N$ as written.

\emph{The integrand.} By rotation invariance it is the same at every point. By \cref{lem:kacrice:regression} the gradient is independent of $(u,A)$, so conditioning on $g=0$ leaves their law unchanged, and $g$ has density $(2\pi p)^{-(N-1)/2}$ at $0$. With $g_k(t)=\E[\abs{\det(A-t)}\ind\{i(A-t)=k\}]$ and $\abs{S_N}=N^{(N-1)/2}2\pi^{N/2}/\Gamma(N/2)$,
\[
\E\kacriceCrt_{N,k}(B)=\frac{\abs{S_N}(p(p-1))^{\frac{N-1}2}}{(2\pi p)^{\frac{N-1}2}}\int_B\sqrt{\tfrac N{2\pi}}\,e^{-\frac N2u^2}g_k\bigl(t(u)\bigr)\dd u.
\]
\emph{The determinant.} Substitute $t=t(u)$: $\dd u=\sqrt{(p-1)/p}\,\dd t$ and $\frac N2u^2=\frac N4t^2+Nat^2$. \Cref{lem:kacrice:extra} with $\varphi(t)=e^{-Nat^2}\ind\{t\in t(B)\}$ turns the integral into $\sqrt{(p-1)/p}\,\frac{c_N}N\E\varphi(\lambda_{(k)})$. The factors that depend on $p$ combine to $(p(p-1))^{\frac{N-1}2}p^{-\frac{N-1}2}\sqrt{(p-1)/p}=(p-1)^{N/2}/\sqrt p$, so $\E\kacriceCrt_{N,k}(B)=\gamma_N(p-1)^{N/2}p^{-1/2}\E\varphi(\lambda_{(k)})$, with $\gamma_N$ independent of $p$, $k$ and $B$.

\emph{The constant.} For $p=2$, $B=\R$, summed over $k$, the right side is $\gamma_N2^{-1/2}N$ and the left side is $2N$ by \cref{lem:kacrice:two}. Hence $\gamma_N=2\sqrt2$, which is \eqref{eq:kacrice:exact}; summing it over $k$ with \cref{lem:kacrice:extra} gives the second formula.
\end{proof}

For $p=2$ the formula says $\E\kacriceCrt_{N,k}(B)=2\Prob(\lambda_{(k)}\in\sqrt2B)$: the critical values of $\ip\sigma{B\sigma}$ are the eigenvalues of $W/\sqrt2$, each twice. Selberg's integral gives the same constant directly (\cref{ex:kacrice:mehta}).\sidenote{\Textcite{Fyodorov2004} reduced the mean count of an isotropic landscape to a mean absolute determinant of a GOE-type matrix and evaluated it at finite $N$; the extra-eigenvalue identity is Lemma~3.3 of \textcite{AuffingerBenArousCerny2013}.}

\begin{theorem}[the annealed complexity]\label{thm:kacrice:annealed}
Let $p\ge2$, $\ell_p=\frac12\log(p-1)$, $c_p=\frac{p-2}{4(p-1)}$ and $I_1(u)=J(2\abs u/\kacriceinf)$. For every $u\in\R$, $\frac1N\log\E\kacriceCrt_N((-\infty,u))\to\Theta_p(u)$ and $\frac1N\log\E\kacriceCrt_{N,0}((-\infty,u))\to\Theta_{0,p}(u)$, where
\begin{gather*}
\Theta_p(u)=\begin{cases}\ell_p-c_pu^2-I_1(u),&u\le-\kacriceinf,\\\ell_p-c_pu^2,&-\kacriceinf\le u\le0,\\\ell_p,&u\ge0,\end{cases}\\
\Theta_{0,p}(u)=\begin{cases}\ell_p-c_pu^2-I_1(u),&u\le-\kacriceinf,\\\ell_p-\frac{p-2}p,&u\ge-\kacriceinf.\end{cases}
\end{gather*}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The plan is to evaluate the exponential rates of the expectations in \eqref{eq:kacrice:exact} by bounding the weight $e^{-Nat^2}$ on the event that matters, using \eqref{eq:kacrice:ldp}. Let $\tau=t(u)$, so that $a\tau^2=c_pu^2$, $J(-\tau)=I_1(u)$ for $u\le-\kacriceinf$, and $4a=\frac{p-2}p$. Since $\frac1N\log(2\sqrt{2/p}\,N(p-1)^{N/2})\to\ell_p$, \cref{thm:kacrice:exact} reduces the claims to the limits of $\frac1N\log Q_N$ and $\frac1N\log Q^0_N$, where
\begin{gather*}
Q_N=\int_{-\infty}^\tau e^{-Nat^2}\rho_N(t)\dd t=\E\Bigl[\frac1N\sum_ie^{-Na\lambda_i^2}\ind\{\lambda_i<\tau\}\Bigr],\\
Q^0_N=\E\bigl[e^{-Na\lambda_{(0)}^2}\ind\{\lambda_{(0)}<\tau\}\bigr].
\end{gather*}
\emph{All critical points, upper bounds.} If $\tau\ge0$, then $Q_N\le1$. If $\tau<0$, every term with $\lambda_i<\tau$ has $\lambda_i^2>\tau^2$, and such a term exists only if $\lambda_{(0)}<\tau$, so $Q_N\le e^{-Na\tau^2}\Prob(\lambda_{(0)}<\tau)$. For $-2\le\tau<0$ we bound the probability by $1$; for $\tau<-2$, \eqref{eq:kacrice:ldp} for $-\lambda_{(0)}$ gives $\limsup\frac1N\log Q_N\le-a\tau^2-J(-\tau)$.

\emph{All critical points, lower bounds.} If $-2<\tau\le0$ and $0<\delta<\tau+2$, the weight is at least $e^{-Na(\tau-\delta)^2}$ on $(\tau-\delta,\tau)$, so $Q_N\ge e^{-Na(\tau-\delta)^2}\E\mu_N((\tau-\delta,\tau))$, and $\E\mu_N((\tau-\delta,\tau))\to\sigma_{\mathrm{sc}}((\tau-\delta,\tau))>0$ by the semicircle law (\cref{thm:toolkit:semicircle}) and bounded convergence. If $\tau>0$, the interval $(-\delta,0)$ serves in the same way. If $\tau\le-2$, then on $\{\tau-\delta<\lambda_{(0)}<\tau\}$ the sum in $Q_N$ is at least $\frac1Ne^{-Na(\tau-\delta)^2}$, so $Q_N\ge\frac1Ne^{-Na(\tau-\delta)^2}\Prob(-\tau<-\lambda_{(0)}<-\tau+\delta)$, and \eqref{eq:kacrice:ldp} gives $\liminf\frac1N\log Q_N\ge-a(\tau-\delta)^2-J(-\tau)$. Letting $\delta\to0$ matches the upper bounds.

\emph{Minima.} For $\tau\le-2$ the same two estimates hold for $Q^0_N$. For $\tau>-2$ and $0<\delta<\min(2,\tau+2)$, split according to whether $\lambda_{(0)}\le-2+\delta$, where $\lambda_{(0)}^2\ge(2-\delta)^2$:
\[
Q^0_N\le e^{-Na(2-\delta)^2}+\Prob\bigl(\lambda_{\max}(W)<2-\delta\bigr),
\]
and the last term is negligible by \eqref{eq:kacrice:ldp}, so $\limsup\frac1N\log Q^0_N\le-a(2-\delta)^2$. Conversely, $(-2-\delta,-2)\subset(-\infty,\tau)$ gives $Q^0_N\ge e^{-Na(2+\delta)^2}\Prob(2<-\lambda_{(0)}<2+\delta)$, and $\liminf\frac1N\log Q^0_N\ge-a(2+\delta)^2-J(2)$, with $J(2)=0$. Letting $\delta\to0$ gives $-4a$.
\end{proof}

Both functions are continuous and nondecreasing. On $u\le0$, $\Theta_p(u)$ is also the rate of the number of critical points near $u$, the physicist's $\Sigma(u)$, and below the threshold $\Theta_{0,p}=\Theta_p$: almost every critical point below $-\kacriceinf$ is a minimum. The weight $e^{-Nat^2}$ favors critical values near $0$, the eigenvalue's entropy favors the bulk, and the threshold is where the eigenvalue leaves the bulk.

\begin{corollary}[the threshold and the ground state]\label{cor:kacrice:groundstate}
Let $p\ge3$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every $u>-\kacriceinf$, $\frac1N\log\E\kacriceCrt_{N,0}((u,\infty))\to-\infty$.
\item $\Theta_{0,p}$ is strictly increasing on $(-\infty,-\kacriceinf]$ and has a unique zero $-E_0$ there, $E_0>\kacriceinf$; almost surely $\liminf_N\kacriceGS_N\ge-E_0$, where $\kacriceGS_N=\frac1N\min_{S_N}H_N$.\index{ground state!spherical p-spin@spherical $p$-spin}
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The plan is to read~(i) off the exact formula and to get~(ii) from Markov's inequality. (i) By \eqref{eq:kacrice:exact} with $B=(u,\infty)$, the mean is at most $2\sqrt{2/p}\,(p-1)^{N/2}$ times $\Prob(\lambda_{(0)}>t(u))=\Prob(\lambda_{\max}(W)<-t(u))$, where $-t(u)<2$, and \eqref{eq:kacrice:ldp} applies. (ii) On $u<-\kacriceinf$, $-c_pu^2$ is strictly increasing for $p\ge3$, and $I_1(u)=J(\abs{t(u)})$ decreases in $u$ because $J$ increases on $[2,\infty)$; so $\Theta_{0,p}$ increases strictly there and tends to $-\infty$ as $u\to-\infty$. At $-\kacriceinf$ it equals $\ell_p-\frac{p-2}p$, which is $0.0132$ at $p=3$ and has the derivative $\frac{(p-2)^2}{2p^2(p-1)}\ge0$ in $p$; so the zero $-E_0$ exists, is unique and lies below $-\kacriceinf$. A global minimizer of $H_N$ is a critical point of index $0$, so for $\eps>0$, by Markov's inequality and \cref{thm:kacrice:annealed},
\[
\Prob\bigl(\kacriceGS_N<-E_0-\eps\bigr)\le\E\kacriceCrt_{N,0}\bigl((-\infty,-E_0-\eps)\bigr)=e^{N(\Theta_{0,p}(-E_0-\eps)+o(1))}.
\]
The exponent is negative, the bounds are summable, and the Borel--Cantelli lemma gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{What the general theorems add}\label{sec:kacrice:general}

\begin{theorem}[fixed index; \textcite{AuffingerBenArousCerny2013}, Theorems~2.5, 2.14 and~2.15]\label{thm:kacrice:index}
For $p\ge2$ and fixed $k\ge0$, $\frac1N\log\E\kacriceCrt_{N,k}((-\infty,u))\to\Theta_{k,p}(u)$, which is $\Theta_{0,p}$ with $(k+1)I_1$ in place of $I_1$; with $-E_k$ its zero, $E_0>E_1>\dots\to\kacriceinf$. The probability of a critical point of index $k$ above $-\kacriceinf+\eps$ is at most $e^{-cN^2}$, and that of one of index at least $k$ below $-E_k-\eps$ decays exponentially.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proofsketch}
Replace $\lambda_{(0)}$ by $\lambda_{(k)}$ in the proof of \cref{thm:kacrice:annealed}: by Theorem~A.1 of \textcite{AuffingerBenArousCerny2013}, the $(k+1)$-th smallest eigenvalue obeys a large deviation principle with rate $(k+1)J(-\,\cdot)$, because $k+1$ eigenvalues must leave the bulk. The bound $e^{-cN^2}$ uses the large deviation principle of the empirical measure at speed $N^2$ (\cref{rem:toolkit:goeldp}) in place of \cref{cor:kacrice:groundstate}(i); the last claim is Markov's inequality. Omitted: the principle for $\lambda_{(k)}$.
\end{proofsketch}

\begin{theorem}[the typical count; \textcite{Subag2017a}, Theorems~1 and~3, Corollary~2]\label{thm:kacrice:subag}
Let $p\ge3$. For $u\in(-E_0,-\kacriceinf)$, $\E[\kacriceCrt_N((-\infty,u))^2]/(\E\kacriceCrt_N((-\infty,u)))^2\to1$, so the count divided by its mean tends to $1$ in $L^2$ and in probability. For $u>-E_0$ the two moments agree on the exponential scale.\index{second moment method}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proofsketch}
A two-point Kac--Rice formula writes the second moment as an integral over pairs of points organized by their overlap; given both gradients vanish, the two Hessians are correlated Gaussian matrices whose determinants Subag controls. Below the threshold, pairs with overlap away from $0$ contribute exponentially less than $(\E\kacriceCrt_N)^2$, and nearly orthogonal pairs match it up to $1+o(1)$. Omitted: the joint law of the two Hessians and the control of every range of overlaps (Sections~5--8 and Appendix~II of \textcite{Subag2017a}).
\end{proofsketch}

With \cref{cor:kacrice:groundstate}, the second moment gives $\kacriceGS_N\to-E_0$ almost surely for every $p\ge3$ \parencite[Appendix~IV]{Subag2017a}; \textcite[Theorem~2.12]{AuffingerBenArousCerny2013} had proved it for even $p\ge4$ through the Parisi formula for spherical models \parencite{Talagrand2006b}, and formula~(1.7) of \textcite{JagannathLopattoMiolane2020} gives the same $E_0(3)$ to twelve digits. Above the threshold \textcite{SubagZeitouni2021} prove concentration for $p\ge32$. For mixed models the mean can mislead: in some, the mean number of minima below an energy grows exponentially while the probability that a critical value lies below it tends to zero \parencite[Corollary~4.1]{AuffingerBenArous2013}. Kac--Rice also bounds the TAP complexity of $\Z_2$ synchronization \parencite[Theorem~1.1]{FanMeiMontanari2021}, placing its low-energy critical points near the Bayes posterior for strong signals (\cref{ch:amp}). For even $p\ge4$, an algorithm with Lipschitz dependence on the couplings reaches energy below $-\kacriceinf-\eps$ only with exponentially small probability \parencite[Theorem~5 and Proposition~2.2 of arXiv v2]{HuangSellke2025}: for such algorithms the threshold is the limit.

\section{The proof, continued: the spiked tensor}\label{sec:kacrice:spiked}

\begin{proposition}[the spiked count at finite $N$; \textcite{BenArousMeiMontanariNica2019}, Lemmas~2--3]\label{prop:kacrice:spiked}
Let $p\ge3$, $\lambda\ge0$, and let $\kacriceCrt_{N,\lambda}(M,B)$ count the critical points of $H_{N,\lambda}$ with $m(\sigma)\in M$ and $H_{N,\lambda}(\sigma)/N\in B$, for Borel sets $M\subset(-1,1)$, $B\subset\R$. With $\theta(m)=\lambda\sqrt{p(p-1)}\,m^{p-2}(1-m^2)$ and a fixed unit vector $e\in\R^{N-1}$,
\begin{multline*}
\E\kacriceCrt_{N,\lambda}(M,B)=K_N\int_M(1-m^2)^{\frac{N-3}2}e^{-\frac N2p\lambda^2m^{2p-2}(1-m^2)}\\
\times\int_Be^{-\frac N2(u+\lambda m^p)^2}\,\E\bigl\lvert\det\bigl(A-t(u)-\theta(m)ee^\top\bigr)\bigr\rvert\dd u\dd m,
\end{multline*}
with $K_N=\abs{S_N}\frac{\Gamma(N/2)}{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma((N-1)/2)}\bigl(\frac{p-1}{2\pi}\bigr)^{\frac{N-1}2}\sqrt{\frac N{2\pi}}$. For local minima, insert the indicator of $A-t(u)-\theta(m)ee^\top\succ0$ in the expectation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The plan is the regression of \cref{lem:kacrice:regression}, shifted by the deterministic spike. We apply the Kac--Rice formula to $H_{N,\lambda}$, a centered Gaussian field plus the smooth function $-\lambda Nm^p$, as \textcite[Lemmas~1--3]{BenArousMeiMontanariNica2019} do. At a point with $m(\sigma)=m$, the spike $F_s(\sigma)=-\lambda N^{1-p}\ip\sigma{x^*}^p$ has gradient $-\lambda pm^{p-1}x^*$, Hessian $-\lambda p(p-1)m^{p-2}x^*x^{*\top}/N$, and $\ip\sigma{\nabla F_s}=-\lambda pNm^p$. The tangential part of $x^*$ has norm $\sqrt{N(1-m^2)}$; call its direction $e$. By \eqref{eq:kacrice:hess}, along the sphere $F_s$ has gradient $-\lambda pm^{p-1}\sqrt{N(1-m^2)}\,e$ and Hessian $-\lambda p(p-1)m^{p-2}(1-m^2)ee^\top+\lambda pm^pI$. The gradient of $H_{N,\lambda}$ vanishes when $g\sim\Normal(0,pI_{N-1})$ equals $\lambda pm^{p-1}\sqrt{N(1-m^2)}\,e$, which has density $(2\pi p)^{-(N-1)/2}e^{-Np\lambda^2m^{2p-2}(1-m^2)/2}$, and conditioning on it leaves the value and the second derivatives of $H_N$ unchanged. The energy $u=H_N/N-\lambda m^p$ has density $\sqrt{N/(2\pi)}\,e^{-N(u+\lambda m^p)^2/2}$. The Hessian is
\[
\sqrt{p(p-1)}A-p(u+\lambda m^p)-\lambda p(p-1)m^{p-2}(1-m^2)ee^\top+\lambda pm^p,
\]
which is $\sqrt{p(p-1)}\bigl(A-t(u)-\theta(m)ee^\top\bigr)$, and by the rotation invariance of $A$ we may fix $e$. Finally, $m$ pushes the surface measure of $S_N$ forward to $\abs{S_N}\frac{\Gamma(N/2)}{\sqrt\pi\Gamma((N-1)/2)}(1-m^2)^{(N-3)/2}\dd m$, the law of one coordinate of a uniform point of the unit sphere; collect the constants as in \cref{thm:kacrice:exact}.
\end{proof}

At $m=0$ and $p\ge3$ the spike drops out of the integrand: the equator does not see the signal.\index{spiked tensor model}

\begin{theorem}[the landscape of the spiked tensor; \textcite{BenArousMeiMontanariNica2019}, Theorems~1--2 and Propositions~1--2, in our normalization]\label{thm:kacrice:bammn}
Let $p\ge3$ and $\lambda\ge0$ be fixed. For Borel sets $M\subset[-1,1]$, $B\subset\R$, $\frac1N\log\E\kacriceCrt_{N,\lambda}(M,B)$ has $\liminf$ at least $\sup\Sigma_\lambda$ over $M^\circ\times B^\circ$ and $\limsup$ at most $\sup\Sigma_\lambda$ over $\bar M\times\bar B$, with $\Sigma_\lambda$ of \eqref{eq:kacrice:sigmalambda}. The same holds for local minima with $\Sigma_\lambda(m,u)-L(\theta(m),-t(u))$, where $L(\theta,s)=+\infty$ for $s<2$; $L(\theta,s)=\frac12J(s)-\frac12J(b)-\frac\theta2(s-b)+\frac18(s^2-b^2)$ for $\theta>1$, $2\le s<b=\theta+\frac1\theta$; and $L=0$ otherwise. For $0\le m<m_c=((p-2)/(\lambda\sqrt{p(p-1)}))^{1/p}$, $S(m)=\sup_u\Sigma_\lambda(m,u)$ is
\[
S(m)=\ell_p+\tfrac12\log(1-m^2)-\tfrac p2\lambda^2m^{2p-2}(1-m^2)+\tfrac{p\lambda^2}{2(p-2)}m^{2p};
\]
on $[m_c,1)$, $S\le0$, with equality exactly at the largest root $m^*$ of $m^{2p-4}(1-m^2)=1/(p\lambda^2)$, which exists if and only if $\lambda\ge\lambda_c(p)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proofsketch}
By \cref{prop:kacrice:spiked} the count is a Laplace integral in $(m,u)$, whose explicit factors give the first four terms of $\Sigma_\lambda$, while $\abs{S_N}$ and the constants give $\frac12(1+\log(p-1))$. A rank-one perturbation changes the determinant by the factor $\abs{1-\theta\ip e{(A-t)^{-1}e}}$, which is not exponential, so the determinant contributes $\Phi(t)$; for minima the outlier must be pushed above $t$, at the cost $L$ from the large deviations of the largest eigenvalue of a rank-one perturbation of the GOE \parencite{Maida2007}. Omitted: uniformity in $(m,u)$ and control of the determinant near the outlier (Section~4 of \textcite{BenArousMeiMontanariNica2019}); the formulas for $S$ are calculus (their Appendix~B).
\end{proofsketch}

The equatorial band is a theorem about means: $S(0)=\ell_p$, and the complexity of minima at $m=0$ is $\Theta_{0,p}(-\kacriceinf)$, for every $\lambda$. The estimator itself is understood exactly.

\begin{theorem}[the maximum likelihood estimator; \textcite{JagannathLopattoMiolane2020}, Theorem~1.2]\label{thm:kacrice:jlm}
Let $p\ge3$, $f_\lambda(q)=\lambda^2q^p+\log(1-q)+q$ on $[0,1)$, $\kacriceML=\sup\{\lambda:\sup_qf_\lambda(q)\le0\}$, and $q_*(\lambda)=0$ for $\lambda<\kacriceML$, $q_*(\lambda)=\arg\max f_\lambda$ for $\lambda>\kacriceML$. If $\hat\sigma$ minimizes $H_{N,\lambda}$ on $S_N$, then $\abs{m(\hat\sigma)}\to\sqrt{q_*(\lambda)}$ in probability for every $\lambda\ne\kacriceML$.
\end{theorem}

Stationarity of $f_\lambda$ reads $q^{p-2}(1-q)=1/(p\lambda^2)$, so above $\kacriceML$ the estimator's overlap is the location $m^*$ of the good critical point; at $\kacriceML$ it jumps from $0$ to $m^*(\kacriceML)$. For $p=3$, $\kacriceML=1.20656$ ($q=0.645007$) and $m^*(\kacriceML)=0.80312$.\sidenote{For $p=2$ the estimator is the top eigenvector, whose overlap grows continuously from $0$ at the BBP threshold (\cref{ch:spiked}); \textcite{JagannathLopattoMiolane2020} stress that for $p\ge3$ it jumps.} Both thresholds agree with the replica predictions of \textcite{RosEtAl2019}: $\lambda_c(3)=1.15470$ and $\kacriceML(3)=1.20656$ against their $r_c=\sqrt6$ and $r_{\mathrm{1ST}}\approx2.56$, that is $1.2068$. Between $\lambda_c$ and $\kacriceML$ the good minimum exists but is not the deepest; between the statistical and the algorithmic thresholds lies the equatorial band. For $\lambda=N^\alpha$ with $\alpha<\frac{p-2}2$, Langevin dynamics at positive temperature, started from the Gibbs measure of the noise alone restricted to $m>0$, reaches overlap $2N^{-1/2+\eps}$ within time $e^{cN^\eps}$ only with probability exponentially small in a power of $N$ \parencite[Theorem~2.10]{BenArousGheissariJagannath2020}; their proof locates the obstruction in the weak curvature of the spike near the equator rather than in the count of minima.

\section{Measuring it}\label{sec:kacrice:measuring}

Five scripts test the formulas; the first, \texttt{13-kacrice-complexity.py}, computes every constant and curve of the chapter.

\paragraph{The exact formula at small $N$.} \texttt{13-kacrice-count.py} finds every critical point of the $3$-spin on $S_N$, $N=3,\dots,7$, by Newton's method on $\nabla H_N=\mu\sigma$, $\norm\sigma^2=N$, from the endpoints of a gradient-descent sweep and then from random points, until three batches in a row find no new critical value; every instance satisfies the Morse identity $\sum_k(-1)^k\kacriceCrt_{N,k}(\R)=1+(-1)^{N-1}$. The averages agree with \eqref{eq:kacrice:exact}, evaluated with $400000$ GOE matrices (\cref{fig:kacrice:count}(a)), also index by index: at $N=7$ the formula gives $4.83$, $10.69$, $15.72$, $17.71$ critical points of indices $0$ to $3$, the count $4.84$, $10.60$, $15.58$, $17.63$. Without the sweep, random-start Newton missed about $6\%$ of the minima at $N=7$, which the Morse identity cannot detect when a minimum and a saddle of index $1$ are missed together.

\paragraph{Where the minima are at finite $N$.} The exponent $\Theta_{0,3}(-\kacriceinf)=0.0132$ is small: at $N=128$ the formula gives only $455$ minima on average. Integrated against $g(u)=u$, \eqref{eq:kacrice:exact} gives the mean energy of a local minimum in the expected count, $\E\sum_{\text{minima}}H_N/N$ over $\E\kacriceCrt_{N,0}(\R)$, as a ratio of expectations over the smallest GOE eigenvalue, which \texttt{13-kacrice-minima.py} samples with the tridiagonal model of \textcite{DumitriuEdelman2002} and Sturm bisection.\sidenote{The tridiagonal model has exactly the eigenvalue law of the GOE at every $N$, so a sample of the smallest eigenvalue costs $O(N)$ operations per bisection step instead of $O(N^3)$.} It is $-1.319$, $-1.415$, $-1.482$, $-1.525$ and $-1.557$ at $N=16$, $32$, $64$, $128$ and $256$, far above $-\kacriceinf=-1.633$; the share of the expected count below $-\kacriceinf$ falls from $1.8\%$ to $0.02\%$, and the gap to the threshold times $\sqrt N$ stays between $1.21$ and $1.26$. A heuristic explains the rate: for a minimum at $t=-2+x$ the weight $e^{-Nat^2}$ gains $e^{4aNx}$, while the probability that the smallest eigenvalue of $A$ exceeds $-2+x$ costs about $e^{-cN^2x^3}$; the balance is at $x\asymp N^{-1/2}$.

\paragraph{Where descent stops.} \texttt{13-kacrice-descent.py} runs projected gradient descent with step $0.15$ until the gradient falls below $10^{-5}\sqrt N$. The endpoints have mean energy $-1.449$, $-1.511$, $-1.537$, $-1.558$ and $-1.582$ at $N=25$, $50$, $75$, $100$ and $200$: below the exact mean minimum by about $0.06$ at $N=25$ and $0.03$ at $N=200$, as befits minima with large basins, and far from $-\kacriceinf$, though the gap times $\sqrt N$ falls from $0.92$ to $0.73$. Single runs range from $-1.735$ ($N=25$, where the ground state itself fluctuates below $-E_0$) to $-1.181$, and from $-1.620$ to $-1.545$ at $N=200$. At the $N=100$ endpoints the Hessian divided by $\sqrt6$ follows the semicircle of \cref{lem:kacrice:regression} centered at the energy (mean eigenvalue $1.932$ against $-t(\bar u)=1.908$ at the mean energy $\bar u=-1.558$; median largest eigenvalue $3.84$ against the edge $3.91$), but that semicircle reaches down to $-0.09$, while every endpoint has a positive smallest eigenvalue (median $0.079$, range $0.021$ to $0.166$): descent stops at minima as close to marginal as the size allows.

\paragraph{The spiked tensor.} \texttt{13-kacrice-spiked.py} runs the same descent on $H_{N,\lambda}$ at $N=80$ (step $0.3/\lambda$ for $\lambda>2$; \cref{fig:kacrice:spiked}(b)). From warm starts at overlap $0.5$ descent lands on the good minimum: for $\lambda=1.2$, $1.5$, $2$, $3$ and $6$ the final overlap is $0.810$, $0.918$, $0.959$, $0.983$ and $0.996$ against $m^*=0.798$, $0.905$, $0.953$, $0.981$ and $0.995$ (intervals below $0.01$), an excess of at most $0.013$, presumably a finite-size effect. Below $\lambda_c$ warm starts stop at overlaps $0.22$ to $0.60$ ($\lambda=0.9$) and $0.33$ to $0.75$ ($\lambda=1.1$), beyond the band of positive annealed complexity of minima ($m\le0.24$ and $0.34$), where at $\lambda=1.1$ that complexity is only about $-0.001$: $e^{80\Sigma}\approx0.9$, so such minima are not rare at this size. At $\lambda=0.6$ all ten end at $\abs m\le0.27$. From random starts (overlap about $N^{-1/2}=0.11$) descent reaches the spike in no run at $\lambda\le0.9$, in $1$ to $4$ of $10$ for $1.1\le\lambda\le3$, and in $8$ and $7$ of $10$ at $\lambda=4$ and $6$; here $N^{(p-2)/2}=9$.

\section{The status board}\label{sec:kacrice:status}

For the pure spherical $p$-spin, the landscape predictions are theorems for mean counts at every energy and fixed index, and for typical counts below the threshold; the ground state is known for every $p\ge3$. What is open is dynamical: that gradient descent ends at the threshold is a conjecture, proved only for Langevin dynamics of the pure model on dimension-free time scales, in the zero-temperature limit (\cref{ch:dmft}). For the spiked tensor, the typical landscape is a physical prediction and the algorithmic threshold is proved in one direction.

\begin{statusboard}

\statusrow{Typical number $=$ mean number (pure model)}{\provedunder}{below $-\kacriceinf$, $p\ge3$ \parencite[Theorem~1]{Subag2017a}; above, $p\ge32$ \parencite{SubagZeitouni2021}}
\statusrow{Typical number $=$ mean number (mixed models)}{\knownfalse}{full mixtures \parencite[Corollary~4.1]{AuffingerBenArous2013}}

\statusrow{Lowest critical point of index $k$ at $-E_k$}{\conjectured}{\textcite[Remark~2.16]{AuffingerBenArousCerny2013}; layers proved as probability bounds}

\section{Where the translation fails}\label{sec:kacrice:fails}

\paragraph{What was assumed.} The model is pure and the disorder Gaussian. The exact formula rests on Euler's identity, which makes the Hessian at energy $u$ a GOE matrix with a deterministic shift, false for mixed models \parencite{AuffingerBenArous2013}, and on the joint density of GOE eigenvalues. Two 2026 preprints treat other couplings: \textcite{ChenLuSen2026} prove that the annealed complexity is unchanged when each coupling is a strictly subgaussian variable plus an independent nonzero Gaussian part (one step of their proof, they write, \enquote{was suggested by ChatGPT (OpenAI)}), and \textcite{Kim2026} constructs a symmetric bounded law, matching the first $2p$ Gaussian moments, whose mean count in a compact energy window grows at a strictly larger exponential rate. All limits fix $\lambda$, $p$ and the index, so the algorithmic regime $\lambda=N^\alpha$ lies outside the landscape theorems, which moreover count in mean.

\paragraph{Where the physics is wrong, or not yet right.} The annealed count is wrong as a typical count for full mixtures \parencite{AuffingerBenArous2013}. For mixed models part~(iii) fails: integrating the dynamical equations, \textcite{FolenaFranzRicciTersenghi2020} find gradient descent from equilibrated starts going below the threshold energy of the dominant marginal minima. For the spiked tensor, the thermodynamic replica method gives partly incorrect predictions for the landscape, which the replicated Kac--Rice computation corrects \parencite{RosEtAl2019}, and the proof of \textcite{BenArousGheissariJagannath2020} locates the failure of descent for $\lambda\ll N^{(p-2)/2}$ in the weak curvature of the signal near the equator, not in a rugged landscape. At accessible sizes the asymptotic landscape is a poor guide: for $N\le256$ the exact formula places almost all minima above $-\kacriceinf$.

\paragraph{Open problems.} As far as we know, these are open as of October 2026. (1) The typical complexity of the spiked tensor near the isolated minimum and in the band near $\lambda_c$, left open by \textcite{BenArousMeiMontanariNica2019} and expected by \textcite{RosEtAl2019} to be within reach. (2) Concentration of the count above the threshold for $3\le p\le31$; \textcite{SubagZeitouni2021} need $p\ge32$. (3) The energy of the lowest critical point of index $k\ge1$, conjectured to tend to $-E_k$ \parencite[Remark~2.16]{AuffingerBenArousCerny2013}. (4) That gradient flow, not only low-temperature Langevin dynamics, ends at $-\kacriceinf$; \textcite[Remark~1.5]{Sellke2024} explains why his method does not reach it. (5) Failure of descent below $N^{(p-2)/2}$ from the uniform start, conjectured for Langevin dynamics by \textcite[Sections~1 and~2.4]{BenArousGheissariJagannath2020}.

\begin{exercises}
\item \consolidation (The covariances.) Complete the proof of \cref{lem:kacrice:regression}: differentiate $C(x,y)=N^{1-p}\ip xy^p$ three and four times and check that at $x=y=\sqrt Ne_N$ only $\frac{p(p-1)}N(\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}+\delta_{il}\delta_{jk})$ survives.\label{ex:kacrice:cov}

\item \consolidation (Counting with signs.) (a) Show $\E\det(tI-A)=N^{-(N-1)/2}\mathrm{He}_{N-1}(t\sqrt N)$, with $\mathrm{He}_n$ the probabilists' Hermite polynomial. (b) Deduce $\sum_k(-1)^k\E\kacriceCrt_{N,k}(\R)=1+(-1)^{N-1}$ for every $p\ge2$. \hint{$\E\mathrm{He}_{2m}(cZ)=(2m-1)!!\,(c^2-1)^m$ for $Z\sim\Normal(0,1)$.}\label{ex:kacrice:euler}

\item \byhand (Selberg's constant.) Use Mehta's integral
\[
\int_{\R^n}\abs{\Delta(x)}\prod_{i=1}^ne^{-x_i^2/2}\dd x=(2\pi)^{n/2}\prod_{j=1}^n\frac{\Gamma(1+j/2)}{\Gamma(3/2)}
\]
to show $c_N=2\sqrt2\,(2/N)^{N/2}\Gamma(1+\frac N2)$ in \cref{lem:kacrice:extra}, and recover $\gamma_N=2\sqrt2$ without \cref{lem:kacrice:two}.\label{ex:kacrice:mehta}

\item \byhand (The equator and the good point.) (a) For $p\ge3$ show that the spike has no gradient and no Hessian along the sphere at $m=0$, where minima have complexity $\ell_p-\frac{p-2}p$ for every $\lambda$. (b) Show that $m^{2p-4}(1-m^2)$ is maximal at $m^2=\frac{p-2}{p-1}$, deduce \eqref{eq:kacrice:lambdac}, and check $\lambda_c(4)=\sqrt{27}/4$ and $m^*(\lambda_c)=m_c$. (c) For $p=3$ show $m^*(\lambda)^2=\frac12(1+\sqrt{1-4/(3\lambda^2)})$.\label{ex:kacrice:mstar}

\item \byhand (The estimator's threshold.) Show that $\kacriceML$ of \cref{thm:kacrice:jlm} is $(pq^{p-2}(1-q))^{-1/2}$ at the root $q\in(0,1)$ of $\frac{q^2}{p(1-q)}+q+\log(1-q)=0$, and compute $\kacriceML(3)=1.20656$, $\kacriceML(4)=1.40567$.\label{ex:kacrice:jlm}

\item \translate (No minima above the threshold, at speed $N^2$.) Using the large deviation principle of the empirical measure at speed $N^2$ (\cref{rem:toolkit:goeldp}), prove $\Prob(\lambda_{(0)}(W)>-2+\delta)\le e^{-c_\delta N^2}$, deduce that a local minimum above $-\kacriceinf+\eps$ has probability at most $e^{-cN^2}$, and say which sentence of the physicist's argument this replaces.\label{ex:kacrice:n2}

\item \translate (Quenched equals annealed.) Let random variables $X_N\ge0$ satisfy $\E X_N^2\sim(\E X_N)^2$. Show that $X_N/\E X_N\to1$ in probability and that $\Prob(X_N>0)\ge(\E X_N)^2/\E X_N^2$; with \cref{thm:kacrice:subag}, prove that $\kacriceGS_N\to-E_0$ in probability. Which replica assumption does each step replace?\label{ex:kacrice:second}

\item \translate (TAP states.) Show that stationarity of \eqref{eq:kacrice:tap} in $q$ at energy $h=H_N(\sigma)/N$ reads $\beta z=\frac{-h\pm\sqrt{h^2-\kacriceinf^2}}{\sqrt2(p-1)}$ with $z=(1-q)q^{p/2-1}$ \parencite[Section~6]{AuffingerBenArousCerny2013}, so that TAP states exist only below the threshold.\label{ex:kacrice:tap}

\item \measurement (Two scalings.) Modify the spiked-tensor script to find, for $N=40$, $80$ and $160$, the $\lambda$ at which half of the random starts reach $m>\frac12$, and compare its growth with $N^{(p-2)/2}$; modify the script for the minima to test whether the gap of the mean minimum to the threshold, times $\sqrt N$, stays near $1.2$ up to $N=1024$.\label{ex:kacrice:escape}

\item \research (The typical spiked landscape.) Compute the second moment of the number of critical points of $H_{N,\lambda}$ near the equator for $\lambda<\lambda_c$, and decide whether the band of minima there is typical, as \textcite{RosEtAl2019} predict.\label{ex:kacrice:research}

The physics of the complexity grew from counting TAP states: \textcite{KurchanParisiVirasoro1993}, \textcite{CrisantiSommers1995} and \textcite{CavagnaGiardinaParisi1998} counted them for the spherical $p$-spin, \textcite{KurchanLaloux1996} found the layers of saddles, and \textcite{CugliandoloKurchan1993} the threshold in the dynamics.

The rigorous theory is due to \textcite{AuffingerBenArousCerny2013}, extended to mixed models by \textcite{AuffingerBenArous2013}; Subag's second moment reaches the ground state without the Parisi formula. The measurements are new.

\paragraph{Translating the literature.} \Textcite{AuffingerBenArousCerny2013} and \textcite{Subag2017a} use our $H_N$, $\kacriceCrt_{N,k}$, $\Theta_p$, $\Theta_{k,p}$, $E_k$ and $\kacriceinf$, with a GOE of variances $(1+\delta_{ij})/(2N)$: their $\lambda_k^N$ is our $\lambda_{(k)}/\sqrt2$. Their Remark~2.7 states the Crisanti--Sommers complexity as $\Theta_{0,p}(2^{-1/2}E)$; it should read $\Theta_{0,p}(\sqrt2E)$, as in their Lemma~6.3, eq.~(6.19), to ten digits by our script (with $2^{-1/2}E$ it would be constant below the threshold). Physicists use energies $E=u/\sqrt2$ (the $3$-spin threshold is $-1.154701$, its ground state $-1.171675$) and temperatures $\sqrt2$ times smaller. \Textcite{BenArousMeiMontanariNica2019} maximize $f=-H_{N,\lambda}/(\sqrt2N)$ on the unit sphere: their $\lambda$ is our $\lambda/\sqrt2$ and their energy $x$ our $-u/\sqrt2$. \Textcite{JagannathLopattoMiolane2020} and \textcite{BenArousGheissariJagannath2020} use our $\lambda$; the former write $\lambda_s$ for our $\lambda_c$ and $\lambda_c$ for our $\kacriceML$. \Textcite{RosEtAl2019} write $r=p\lambda/\sqrt2$; \textcite{HuangSellke2025} and Sellke maximize $H_N$.
