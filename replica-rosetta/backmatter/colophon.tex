\specialchapter{How This Book Was Made}{Colophon}{}

\addsec{Roles}

\emph{The Replica Rosetta Stone} was written by Claude Opus 5.5, an AI model made by Anthropic, in October 2026. Xiaoyu Li conceived the series \emph{The Missing Textbooks} and proposed this volume: its subject, the exact predictions of high-dimensional statistics and learning theory that came from statistical physics, and their proofs; its gap, a parallel text for readers fluent in one of the two languages; its form, the physical derivation and the rigorous route set side by side with a status board in every chapter; the rule that every example come from inference and learning; and the one-language works it should be measured against, among them the introduction of Barbier and Macris to adaptive interpolation and the reviews and lecture notes of Zdeborová and Krzakala. Xiaoyu Li also set the direction of the series (language, design, and credit) and is responsible for the book's publication.

\addsec{Process}

A coordinating instance of the model first surveyed the literature with four research assistants, separate instances that verified, with web search and the papers themselves, the replica method and interpolation with the Nishimori line and the perceptron; the cavity method, message passing and random landscapes; Gaussian comparison and regression; and dynamics, together with the history of the field and the books and notes that already exist. They found that two works already teach replica computations next to rigorous arguments for selected models, the tutorial of Montanari and Sen and the lecture notes of Krzakala and Zdeborová, and the plan was narrowed to claim, as far as the authors know, a systematic parallel text of this kind with a status board for every prediction. Their digests corrected the plan in several places before a chapter was written. The threshold of the Ising teacher--student perceptron that Barbier and coauthors print, $1.249$, does not agree with their own formula, which gives $1.2447$. The often-quoted critical density $0.09$ of sparse PCA mixes two different densities, $0.0964$ and $0.0856$. A margin in a proposition of Altschuler and Tikhomirov is about five times smaller than stated, without affecting their theorem. The arXiv version of a paper on the TAP free energy misprints the formula for the spectral start of message passing. Two papers of Ben Arous, Dembo and Guionnet had been conflated, the volume of the 2022 Les Houches school that the plan would have cited has not, as far as could be verified, appeared, and a cited tutorial had changed its title. And a cluster of preprints from 2026, several of them written, by their authors' accounts, with the help of AI systems, had settled questions about the Sherrington--Kirkpatrick model and the binary perceptron that were open when the plan was conceived; the status word \emph{preprint} was added for them.

The coordinator then wrote the plan (the thesis, the global normalizations, which the two literatures disagree on, fixed labels for results that chapters cite across the book, and a specification for each chapter), a style guide (the anatomy of a chapter, the voice, a proof style that follows Halmos, and the conventions of the scripts), the volume's typography with its stone and status boards, a shared kit for the scripts, and the prologue.

Separate instances of the same model drafted the fifteen chapters and the appendix, up to thirteen at a time. Each verified its references and the claims it attributes to papers, wrote and ran the scripts behind its figures, checked its theorems and constants numerically, and built its chapter with the volume's checks for errors, references, layout, and style. Some authors found that their specifications were wrong, and said so. For generalized linear models the straight interpolation path of the plan has a remainder without a sign, so \cref{ch:glm} replaced the planned one-sided bound by an exact sum rule, a bound that is conditional on the concentration of the overlap, and counting theorems that bracket the threshold of the Ising teacher. \Cref{ch:nishimori} proved the whole replica-symmetric formula for $\Z_2$ synchronization, where the plan had asked for one direction. The reports of six authors were lost when the work was paused, as described below, and their referees checked those chapters against their specifications instead. Some proofs and observations are, as far as the authors and referees could find, the book's own, and the chapters mark them. Among them are the bracket $1\le\alpha_{\mathrm{IT}}\le1.4480$ for the Ising teacher, with exact recovery above the threshold (\cref{ch:glm}); the closed form $(1+\log2)/2$ of an upper bound on the capacity of the binary perceptron, and the crossover $\kappa=-0.6219$ below which Stojnic's lifted bound beats Gardner's formula (\cref{ch:gordon}); a proof of the ridgeless limits of ridge regression by a monotone comparison and the mean of the inverse Wishart matrix (\cref{ch:ridge}); the slicing of Gardner's volume by the teacher (\cref{ch:classification}); and the comparison on the circle in which the Gaussian approximation to a kernel learning curve fails at zero ridge (\cref{ch:kernels}).

Each chapter, and the prologue and the appendix together, then received one independent referee pass from a separate instance, which re-derived the results line by line and checked them numerically, reran the scripts and compared every printed number with the text, worked the exercises, and checked the claims about papers against the papers; it corrected what it found directly. No referee found a false main theorem, except in one degenerate case: at zero signal, where its maximizer is not unique (\cref{ch:nishimori}). The referees found real errors elsewhere. Some cited theorems had been stated with the wrong hypotheses: a step size of order $d^{-1/2}$ where the source requires order $t^{-1/2}$ (\cref{ch:sgd}), a state-evolution theorem applied to a channel that is not Lipschitz (\cref{ch:glm}), a failure of universality for the LASSO that its authors prove only for least squares (\cref{ch:lasso}), and a theorem on gradient flow that covers only the Bayes risk (\cref{ch:dmft}). Some board rows claimed more than their sources, and three in \cref{ch:glm} became \emph{proved under}; one row of \cref{ch:nishimori} went the other way, from \emph{conjectured} to \emph{proved under}, when its referee found a theorem of Barbier and Panchenko. Credit was wrong in several places: the exact replica moments of the random energy model had been derived by Ogure and Kabashima (\cref{ch:rem}), the plateau formulas of online SGD are in a paper of Saad and Solla from 1995, who also saw the student units separate at a finite learning rate (\cref{ch:sgd}), the ingredients of the circle example were in print (\cref{ch:kernels}), and the uniqueness of the maximizer in $\Z_2$ synchronization is due to Deshpande, Abbe and Montanari (\cref{ch:nishimori}). The margin below which Stojnic's lifted bound proves Gardner's formula wrong, quoted as $-0.63$ from a paper that states no threshold, is $-0.622$ (\cref{ch:gordon}), and the jump of a sparse prior at density $0.05$, printed as $0.585$ in one chapter, is $0.583$ (\cref{ch:nishimori,ch:spiked}). Many theorem numbers had been taken from arXiv versions that differ from the published papers; the referees corrected those they could check and say which version the others come from. Figures had points off the plot (\cref{ch:ridge}), a curve drawn against the wrong axis and a legend that hid data (\cref{ch:classification}); two confidence intervals were corrected (\cref{ch:sgd}); a side note blamed undercovering intervals for an outlier that is a rare fluctuation (\cref{ch:nishimori}); a cut that the pause had interrupted was repaired (\cref{ch:dmft}); and a compressed revision had dropped a qualification of Sellke's theorem (\cref{ch:kacrice}). Each of these errors is corrected in the text. The book also reports errors in the sources, found by the research assistants, the authors, or the referees, and confirmed by the referees: a threshold printed as $1.249$ that the paper's own formula puts at $1.2447$ (\cref{ch:glm}), a proposition whose margin is about five times smaller than stated, and a figure caption that contradicts its paper's own definition (\cref{ch:gordon}), misprints in arXiv versions of papers on message passing (\cref{ch:amp,ch:spiked}), a reversed inequality (\cref{ch:classification}), a parameter defined in two inconsistent ways (\cref{ch:kernels}), and a misprinted remark on the complexity of the spherical spin glass (\cref{ch:kacrice}). The chapters state each of them where the source is used.

A usage limit stopped the running agents once, and each was resumed with its context. Later the work was paused, at the editor's request, while several authors and referees were still at work, and their contexts did not survive the pause. When the work resumed in a new session, the state of each chapter was reconstructed from its files, and every referee pass that had not finished was run again, from the start or, where the earlier referee had already edited the chapter, as a pass that checked those edits and completed the review, with the earlier referee's scratch computations available to it.

While the chapters were being refereed, the coordinator wrote the preface, the notation, and the epilogue, whose figure is computed from the chapters' status boards by a script, and revised them as the referee reports came in. It then integrated the book: it reconciled numbers and notation between chapters, gave every work cited in several chapters a single bibliographic entry, and checked the whole book for layout, undefined references, and overflowing lines. A last referee pass checked the epilogue, the preface, the notation, this colophon, and the volume's README against the chapters.

\addsec{Limits}

No human has yet checked every proof or every attribution. Web search and the papers themselves were available while the book was written, but some full texts could not be opened: several classic physics papers are behind paywalls and were checked against their abstracts, against later papers that restate them, or by recomputing their formulas, and the chapters say so where it matters. The preprints of 2026 are reported as their authors state them; the book checked their statements and versions, not their proofs. One referee pass per chapter finds many errors but not all of them, and a referee that is an instance of the same model may share the author's blind spots. Results that the book presents as its own have been checked by the same model only. The scripts are small: exact enumeration reaches sixteen spins, and simulations reach dimensions of a few thousand, which confirms the arithmetic of the theorems and shows finite-size effects, not the behavior of large systems.
