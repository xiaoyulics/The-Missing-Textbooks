\specialchapter{How This Book Was Made}{Colophon}{16}

\addsec{Roles}

\emph{Deep Learning as an Experimental Science} was written by Claude Opus 5.5, an AI model made by Anthropic, in October 2026. Xiaoyu Li conceived the series \emph{The Missing Textbooks} and proposed this volume: its subject, its gap (an experimental science whose method is taught by word of mouth), its format (each chapter opening with a famous wrong conclusion and performing its post-mortem), its first examples, and its position relative to existing books. Xiaoyu Li also set the direction of the series (language, design and credit) and is responsible for the book's publication.

\addsec{Process}

A written plan fixed the thesis, the fifteen famous conclusions, the vocabulary of verdicts, the global notation, the conventions of the laboratory, and a specification for each chapter. A style guide fixed the anatomy of a chapter, the voice, a proof style that follows Halmos, and a rule of fairness: each claim is stated as its authors stated it before it is examined, and a verdict applies to a claim, never to a person. A coordinating instance of the model designed the volume's additions to the series typography, wrote the shared laboratory kit, the prologue, the front matter, the statistical appendix and the epilogue, ran the epilogue's study, and integrated the whole.

Separate instances of the same model drafted the fifteen chapters, five at a time, in three batches. Each verified its references and the claims it attributes to papers with web search, wrote and ran the scripts behind its figures, checked its theorems and constants numerically, and built its chapter with the volume's checks for errors, references, layout and style. The second batch was stopped at its start by a usage limit and resumed, with its context, after the limit reset. One author also had its chapter checked by an independent instance, which found that a coverage estimate had treated dependent forecasts as independent and tightened several attributions; the corrections are in the chapter.

The chapters corrected the plan in several places, among them the direction of a parameter-counting effect and the effect of unfinished learning-rate schedules in \cref{ch:protocol}, the description of proper scoring rules in \cref{ch:emergence}, and the strength of the verdict in \cref{ch:confounding}; where the plan and a chapter differ, the chapter is correct. Several laboratory miniatures did not reproduce parts of the results they were built to show, and each chapter says which.

\addsec{Limits}

No human has yet checked every proof or every attribution, and the chapters have not yet received the independent referee pass that the earlier volumes of the series received. Web search was available while the book was written, but the full texts of many papers could not be opened, so some claims about what a paper reports were checked against abstracts, tables and secondary accounts; the chapters say so where it matters. The laboratory experiments are deliberately small, and a miniature that reproduces a failure does not prove that the failure explains the original result. Results from 2024 to 2026 are cited from preprints where no refereed version could be found. Corrections are welcome and will be recorded in the repository of \emph{The Missing Textbooks}.
