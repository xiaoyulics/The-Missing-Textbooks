\specialchapter{How This Book Was Made}{Colophon}{34}

\addsec{Roles}

\emph{The Hydrogen Atoms of Deep Learning} was written by Claude Opus 5.5, an AI model made by Anthropic, running at its maximum reasoning effort, in October 2026. Xiaoyu Li conceived the series \emph{The Missing Textbooks} and proposed this volume: its subject, its gap (solvable models of deep learning scattered across papers in incompatible notation, and rarely accompanied by an account of where they fail), its format (a phenomenon, a minimal solvable model, a theorem with a complete proof, and a section on what the model gets wrong), a first list of models, and its position relative to existing books. Xiaoyu Li also set the direction of the series (language, design and credit) and is responsible for the book's publication.

\addsec{Process}

A written plan fixed the thesis, the sixteen models, the global notation, and a specification for each chapter, including the editor's plan for each main theorem; several of these plans were checked numerically before writing began. A style guide fixed the anatomy of a chapter, the voice, a proof style that follows Halmos, the three-way distinction between proofs, sketches and heuristic derivations, and the rule that every chapter compares an exact prediction with a simulation. Separate instances of the same model then drafted the chapters in parallel, each re-deriving its results, checking them numerically, verifying its references with web search, and writing the scripts behind its figures. A coordinating instance wrote the prologue, preface, notation and epilogue, designed the volume's additions to the series typography, and integrated the whole.

Each chapter then received at least one independent referee pass from a separate instance, which re-derived the proofs, re-ran the figure scripts, checked constants and exercises numerically, and checked historical claims and references against sources wherever these could be reached. The referees corrected the errors they found directly.

\addsec{Limits}

No human has yet checked every proof. Several results are presented as heuristic derivations, marked with $\Diamond$, because no proof is known to the author or because a proof would not fit; they are checked against simulations but not proved. Some historical details rest on secondary accounts. Results from 2024 to 2026 are cited from preprints where no refereed version could be found, and are marked as such where a claim rests on them. Corrections are welcome and will be recorded in the repository of \emph{The Missing Textbooks}.
