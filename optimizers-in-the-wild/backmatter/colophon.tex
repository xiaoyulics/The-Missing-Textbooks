\specialchapter{How This Book Was Made}{Colophon}{34}

\addsec{Roles}

\emph{Optimizers in the Wild} was written by Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, in October 2026. Xiaoyu Li conceived the series \emph{The Missing Textbooks} and proposed this volume: its subject, its gap (optimization textbooks stop at convex analysis and stationary points, while practice is decided by Adam, warmup, weight decay, learning-rate schedules, Muon and the edge of stability), its two lines (the unification of optimizers as steepest descent under norms with preconditioning and moving averages, and the dynamics of training), and its position relative to existing work. Xiaoyu Li also set the direction of the series (language, design and credit) and is responsible for the book's publication.

\addsec{Process}

A written plan fixed the thesis, the fifteen chapters, the global notation, the labels of the shared appendix, and a specification for each chapter, including the editor's plan for each main theorem. Several of these plans were checked numerically before writing began, and three were corrected as a result. A style guide fixed the anatomy of a chapter, the voice, a proof style that follows Halmos, the distinction between proofs, sketches, heuristic derivations, observations and folklore, and the rule that every chapter compares an exact prediction with a simulation.

Separate instances of the same model then drafted the chapters and the appendix in parallel, each re-deriving its results, checking them numerically, verifying its references by web search, and writing the scripts behind its figures. The plan turned out to be wrong in more places than the numerical checks had found, and the chapters, not the plan, were corrected: among other things, the advantage of a preconditioner at small batch sizes in the noisy quadratic model does not vanish, the residual loss after a cooldown in the river valley falls like the inverse square root of the cooldown's length, the equilibrium angle of a normalized layer under weight decay is $\arccos(1-\eta\lambda)$, the exponent by which Kronecker factors improve conditioning is $\abs{1-qs}$, the curvature of the softmax loss is bounded with the constant $2$, and the bound on Adam's step at $\beta_2=0.95$ is $1.16$ learning rates. The web-search budget shared by the drafting instances ran out before every reference had been checked; the rest were checked later.

Each chapter and the appendix then received one independent referee pass from a separate instance, which re-derived the proofs, re-ran the figure scripts, checked constants and exercises numerically, and checked references and historical claims by web search. The referees corrected what they found directly. They corrected the statements of several results and the steps of several proofs, and they made many more corrections to numbers quoted from simulations, to the statements and answers of exercises, and to claims about what other papers prove, some of which were softened or removed because they could not be confirmed. A usage limit stopped most of the referee passes midway, and each was resumed where it had stopped.

A coordinating instance wrote the prologue, preface, notation, epilogue and this colophon, designed the volume's additions to the series typography, and integrated the whole: it made the conventions for notation and references uniform, checked the references that were still unverified, and rebuilt the book. The prologue and front matter, and the epilogue, were then refereed by separate instances against the finished chapters, and a further instance checked the chapters against each other: their cross-references, the constants that several chapters share, and the notation.

\addsec{Limits}

No human has yet checked every proof. Several results are presented as heuristic derivations, marked with $\Diamond$, because no proof is known to the author or because a proof would not fit; they are checked against simulations but not proved. Web search was available while the book was written, but the full texts of most papers could not be opened, so references were checked against search results, abstracts and listings, and bibliographic details are given only where a search confirmed them. Some historical details, and some translations of the notation of source papers, rest on these or on the author's memory, and the chapters say so where it matters. One referee pass per chapter finds many errors but not all of them. Results from 2025 and 2026 are cited from preprints where no refereed version could be found, and are marked as such where a claim rests on them. Practices change quickly, and the recipes quoted in the book are dated. Corrections are welcome and will be recorded in the repository of \emph{The Missing Textbooks}.
