\specialchapter{How This Book Was Made}{Colophon}{34}

\addsec{Roles}

\emph{Dissolving Problems} was written by Claude Opus 5.5, an AI model made by Anthropic, running at its maximum reasoning effort, in October 2026. Xiaoyu Li conceived the series \emph{The Missing Textbooks}, chose this volume's subject from a list of candidate books, set its direction (language, design, structure and credit), and is responsible for its publication. The book's framework draws on a research guide to Grothendieck's rising sea from Xiaoyu Li's own toolkit. That framework is the requirement that a reframing must shorten proofs, drop assumptions or genuinely unify, together with the advice to stoop low.

\addsec{Process}

The book was planned as a casebook from the start. A written plan fixed the thesis, the seventeen cases and a specification for each chapter. A style guide fixed the anatomy of a case, the voice, and a proof style that follows Halmos. Fifteen instances of the same model then drafted the chapters in parallel. A coordinating instance wrote the prologue, preface and epilogue, designed the typography, and integrated the whole.

Each chapter then received at least one independent referee pass from a separate instance. The referee re-derived the proofs, checked constants and exercises, often by computer, and checked historical claims and references against sources wherever these could be reached. The referees found and corrected real errors. Most were false sentences around correct proofs, missing hypotheses, or mistaken or overstated attributions. Several details of the original plan were also corrected along the way.

\addsec{Limits}

No human has yet checked every proof. Some historical details rest on secondary accounts, because the web search available during writing was limited. Recent preprints are cited as unrefereed claims, notably the announced counterexample to Sidorenko's conjecture and the announced proof of the matroid secretary conjecture, both from September 2026. Corrections are welcome and will be recorded in the repository of \emph{The Missing Textbooks}.
